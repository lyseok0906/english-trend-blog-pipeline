## S2 — TSA local press release, Harrisburg, Nov. 24, 2025 (primary, official; a year old)

- URL: https://www.tsa.gov/news/press/releases/2025/11/24/tsa-prepared-for-busy-thanksgiving-travel-harrisburg-offers-special
- Title: "TSA prepared for busy Thanksgiving travel in Harrisburg, offers special packing tips". Label "Local Press Release", dated Monday, November 24, 2025.
- Verbatim: "Most foods can be carried through a TSA checkpoint, but some items must be transported in checked baggage." "Solid items can go through a checkpoint, but passengers carrying items that can spill, spread, spray, pump, or pour and are larger than 3.4 ounces should pack those in checked luggage."
- Carry-through examples (verbatim): "Baked goods – Homemade or store-bought pies, cakes, cookies, brownies, and other sweet treats." "Meats – Turkey, chicken, ham, steak (frozen, cooked, or uncooked)." "Stuffing – Cooked or uncooked, in a box or bag." "Casseroles – Solid casseroles can be brought in carry-on or checked luggage. However, casseroles with liquid or semi-liquid components (e.g., soups, sauces, or gravy) must comply with TSA's 3-1-1 liquids rule." "Mac 'n Cheese – Must be solid and already cooked in a pan, or passengers may travel with the ingredients to cook at their destination." "Fresh vegetables" and "Fresh fruit" (examples listed, including cranberries), "Candy", "Spices".
- Checked-baggage examples (verbatim): "Cranberry sauce – Homemade or canned, as it is spreadable." "Gravy – Homemade or in a jar/can." "Canned fruits or vegetables – Items in liquid-filled cans or jars should be packed in checked luggage." "Preserves, jams, and jellies – Spreadable items must be checked." "Maple syrup".
- Cold items: "If items need to stay cold during travel, ice packs are permitted but must be frozen solid when going through security screening." Also a food-safety reminder pointing to the FDA (not used).
- Other airports: at least ten local releases of Nov. 24 and 25, 2025 carry the same headline pattern (Allentown, Kentucky, Philadelphia, Pittsburgh, Upstate New York, West Virginia, Cleveland, New Orleans; Miami's reads "unveils faster checkpoint and holiday food packing tips"). Only Harrisburg's text was read in full; the others were not compared.

**Where S1 and S2 differ (to handle in the article).** S1 lists gravy, jam and jelly and maple syrup as carry-on "Yes (Less than or equal to 3.4oz/100 ml allowed)" and checked "Yes"; S2 says to pack spreadable items such as cranberry sauce, gravy and jams in checked luggage. They are consistent for amounts over 3.4 oz (S1: not allowed in carry-on beyond 3.4 oz; S2: checked), but S2 states it as a pack-in-checked rule for the item, while S1 allows small amounts in carry-on. S1 has no cranberry sauce row; S2's cranberry sauce statement has no counterpart on the Food page. S2's "Meats – ... (frozen, cooked, or uncooked)" in carry-on vs S1's "Yes (Special Instructions)" for fresh and frozen meat (ice rule).

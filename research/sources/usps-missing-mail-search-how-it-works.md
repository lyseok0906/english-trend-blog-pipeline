## S1 — USPS FAQ, "Missing Mail - The Basics" (primary, official)

URL https://faq.usps.com/s/article/Missing-Mail-The-Basics. Article Number 000007968; dated **May 19, 2026**.
Definition: "Missing Mail is mail that has not been delivered by the expected delivery date. This applies to packages that are undelivered, lost, or late. If it has been lost, late or missing for 7 days or more, you can:" submit a Missing Mail search request at MissingMail.USPS.com; see the article on the USPS Mail Recovery Center; "File a claim (for insured items meeting the appropriate timeframe)"; visit the local Post Office. Before acting: "please check USPS Tracking before taking the action described below."
Table by service (check USPS Tracking first):
- Priority Mail Express: "If seven (7) or more days have passed since the date of mailing and the recipient has confirmed the item has not arrived, you may either submit a search request for your lost mailpiece and/or email us."
- Priority Mail; First-Class Mail with a trackable service (USPS Tracking, Signature Confirmation, Certified Mail); USPS Ground Advantage; USPS Tracking, Signature Confirmation or Certified Mail services: check the delivery status in USPS Tracking (no wait listed in these rows).
- Domestic Registered Mail: "Please allow fourteen (14) or more days from the date of mailing for your item to arrive. If the proper amount of time has passed and the item has not arrived, you may submit a search request ... on the Missing Mail application and/or file a claim."
- Insurance: check the delivery status using the insurance tracking numbers on the receipt.
- Parcel Select: "Please allow eight (8) days from the date of mailing for your item to arrive. If fourteen (14) or more days have passed since the date of mailing and the recipient has confirmed that the item has not arrived, you may submit a search request ... and/or email us."
Eligibility: "The Postal Service will attempt to find and return your lost item(s), but submitting a search does not guarantee a successful outcome." Not searched for safety: hazardous material; live ammunition; opened hair/beauty products; over the counter drugs; prescription drugs; perishables / food; handguns ("will only be returned to a licensed dealer on your behalf"). The lead-in says "we cannot search for or return the following."
Filing: MissingMail.USPS.com, sign in or register; confirmation; Missing Mail Search History; drafts "expire after 7 days"; "Customer Care representatives are not able to submit a missing mail search request for you."
Needed: mailer and recipient addresses; date of mailing; tracking numbers ("highly recommended, if available"); type of container, size and color (example "12 x 10 brown box"); detailed description of contents of each item; attach photos if available.
Window: earliest "seven days after the original date of mailing"; latest "365 days after the original date of mailing"; "We strongly suggest that you submit your search request as soon as possible within the 7 to 365 day window."
Status and results: emails to the address in "Whom can we contact about this search?"; final resolution email for return or expiration. Statuses: Accepted (received), Mailpiece Found, Expired ("reached its retention period" with no length given), Saved as Draft, Rejected (by a clerk, for example as a duplicate), Canceled. Found: rewrapped and sent to the address on the completed form. Not found: "A search will continue to run ... until it expires."
Multiple items: several items inside one mailpiece in one request; separate mailpieces need separate requests. Edit: contents of the mailpiece only ("you will not be allowed to edit any other information"); otherwise cancel and submit a new request. Cancel: from Search History with the "X" or the Cancel option; "No further matches will be attempted and no further email notifications will be sent."
Related articles listed (not read): Domestic Claims - The Basics; PO Box - The Basics; USPS Hold Mail - The Basics; U.S. Passports - The Basics; Change of Address - The Basics.
Absence tests (article body only): no "fee", "free", "charge", "$" or "%"; the only "cost" is "includes tracking in the cost of mailing"; no length given for the retention period; no sentence linking the 7-day application minimum to the 14-day table rows.

## S2 — USPS.com, "Find Missing Mail" (primary, official)

URL https://www.usps.com/help/missing-mail.htm. No update date; an ALERT banner about application maintenance on Nov. 12 is not used.
Step 1: "if your package or mail has tracking, check USPS Tracking to see its current status"; Informed Delivery users: "There may be occasions where a mail item may be delayed in reaching its destination. For additional information, please visit Informed Delivery FAQs."
Step 2: "Missing Mail search requests can be submitted starting 7 days from mailing." Information: sender mailing address; recipient mailing address; size and type of container or envelope; identifying information such as the USPS Tracking number(s), the mailing date from the mailing receipt or Click-N-Ship label receipt; description of the contents (brand, model, color, size); pictures.
Next: a confirmation email and periodic updates; "If we find your package or mail, we will send it to the address you provided. Please note, sometimes we cannot find missing mail items. It is also possible that your package or mail will not be recovered because it was not safe to forward." Link: "Missing Mail Recovery Restrictions" (not read).
Link targets on the page: "Start Your Missing Mail Search" goes to https://missingmail.usps.com/; "Filing a Claim" goes to https://www.usps.com/help/claims.htm.
Requesting a Refund: "If you used a service with a money-back guarantee such as Priority Mail Express, you may be able to request a refund."
File a Claim: "If your mail or package included insurance, you may be able to file an insurance claim. For damaged or missing contents, we recommend filing a claim immediately, but you must file no later than 60 days from the date of mailing." The 60-day sentence begins "For damaged or missing contents" and is limited to that case (Gate 2 FIX on a2f1755).
Absence tests: no "365", no fee or cost, no "fourteen" or "14 days", no "retention", no update date.

## Differences shown in the article (not resolved)

1. FAQ table: allow 14 or more days (Registered Mail, Parcel Select) and 7 or more (Priority Mail Express); the application accepts requests from day 7 (FAQ, USPS.com).
2. FAQ gives the 365-day latest day; USPS.com gives only the 7-day start.
3. Claim timing: the FAQ says "meeting the appropriate timeframe" with no number; USPS.com gives 60 days only for damaged or missing contents. The article does not apply the 60 days to every lost item (Gate 2 FIX).
4. The FAQ's list of items USPS "cannot search for or return" includes handguns, with a note that they are returned only to a licensed dealer.

## S3 — USPS.com, "File a Claim" (primary, official; added after Gate 2 FIX on `a2f1755`, pointer only)

URL https://www.usps.com/help/claims.htm (reached from the "Filing a Claim" link on S2). Fetched and tested 2026-11-13; no update date. Section "Filing Periods": "Each mail service has a different filing period. The filing period is based on the mailing date on your receipt and whether your package arrived damaged." "Damaged or Missing Contents: If your item arrives damaged or is missing contents, you may file a claim immediately but must file no later than 60 days after the mailing date." "Lost Mail or Package: If your item does not arrive at all, file a claim in the time period for the service you used." The page has a table of filing periods by service; none of its values is used. Used only for the two cases above and as the link target for claims.

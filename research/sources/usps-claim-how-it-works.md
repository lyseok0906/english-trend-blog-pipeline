## S1 — USPS.com, "File a Claim" (primary, official)

URL https://www.usps.com/help/claims.htm. No update date shown.
"Either the sender or the recipient may file a claim for insured mail that is lost, arrived damaged, or was missing contents. The person filing must have the original mailing receipt. Each claim must be filed within a certain time period and include proof of insurance, value, and damage." "NOTE: These instructions do not include all the details and exceptions in the Postal Regulations in the Domestic Mail Manual (DMM)." Eligible items: "Priority Mail Express items and other insured mail; Collect on Delivery (COD) items; Registered Mail items with insurance"; "Your insurance may have been included with the USPS service or purchased separately." "(For USPS fee or postage refunds, please see Request a USPS Refund: Domestic.)"
Step 1: "The filing period is based on the mailing date on your receipt and whether your package arrived damaged." Damaged: "If your item arrives damaged or is missing contents, you may file a claim immediately but must file no later than 60 days after the mailing date." Lost: "If your item does not arrive at all, file a claim in the time period for the service you used." Chart (File After / File Before): Priority Mail Express 7 / 60 days; Priority Mail Express COD 15 / 60; Priority Mail 15 / 60; USPS Ground Advantage 15 / 60; Insured Mail 15 / 60; Collect on Delivery (COD) 15 / 60; Registered Mail & Registered COD Mail 15 / 60; APO/FPO Priority Mail Express Military Service 21 days / 180 days; APO/FPO/DPO Insured Mail, Registered Mail (...) 45 days / 1 year; APO/FPO/DPO Insured Mail (Surface only) 75 days / 1 year.
Step 2: tracking or label number "between 13 and 34 characters", found on "online label record, package label, mailing receipt, or sales receipt". Evidence of insurance: original mailing receipt; outer packaging showing the names and addresses of the sender and the addressee and the proper label showing the article was sent insured; printed electronic online label record or computer printout. "USPS may not legally pay compensation for uninsured lost or damaged articles. If your uninsured mail is missing or delayed you may request a Missing Mail Search." Proof of value list (sales receipt; paid invoice or paid bill of sale; statement of value and/or estimates of repair costs from a reputable dealer; credit card billing statement; receipt of costs for reconstruction of non-negotiable documents; printouts of the online transaction ...). Proof of damage: photos; "an estimate of the repair costs from a reputable dealer"; "Save the original packaging and everything in the package until your claim is settled. Do not throw out damaged items, even after you photograph them. Do not reship items that were not damaged." "You may be asked to take the entire package to your local Post Office."
Step 3: "The fastest and easiest way to file your claim is online" (free USPS.com account; save to finish later; Claim History; email notifications); by mail: call USPS National Materials Customer Service to request a Domestic Claim PS Form.
After: "USPS usually sends claims decisions within 5-10 days." Payment "in 7-10 business days". "USPS does not pay a claim higher than an item's actual value." Denial letter "will list the reasons". Appeal: "within 30 days of receiving the decision"; second appeal "within 30 days of the date you received the appeal denial".
Absence tests: no "deleted after", no "10 items", no "Consumer Advocate", no "photocopy", no 2026 date.

## S2 — USPS FAQ, "Domestic Claims - The Basics" (primary, official)

URL https://faq.usps.com/s/article/Domestic-Claims-The-Basics. Article 000006089, dated **Nov 6, 2026**.
Who: "Either the person sending the mailpiece (Mailer) or the person receiving the mailpiece (Recipient or Addressee) may file a claim for a damaged or missing article." If the mailer files for a damaged article in the recipient's possession "the Recipient is required to retain the damaged article and container ... until the claim is fully resolved". "If the Mailer and the Addressee both claim insurance, any payment due is made to the party who submits the claim first and whose claim is approved for payment."
Filing online: "You can add up to 10 items as long as they're included in the same tracking number." "Proof of value and proof of insurance is required." By mail: call National Materials Customer Service.
When: "Damaged or Missing Contents: Customers should file a claim immediately but must file no later than 60 days from the date of mailing." Lost: chart "WHEN TO FILE (FROM MAILING DATE)", columns "No Sooner Than" / "No Later Than": same ten rows as S1 (Priority Mail, USPS Ground Advantage and Insured Mail marked with an asterisk). Footnote: "*Bulk Claims submitted via the Business Customer Gateway (BCG) should be filed no sooner than 15 days." "Source DMM 609.1.4". "A decision regarding most claims is usually communicated within 5-10 days. Claims requiring in-depth processing, such as high claim amounts, can take up to 30 days." "Allow 7-10 business days to receive approved claims payments in the mail." "Saved claims will be deleted after 3 days, if not submitted."
Money: "Postage reimbursements will be included with the claim payment if the item was lost or not delivered. Insurance fees cover the cost of the insurance and are not reimbursed."
Proof of damage / addressee must keep container: "Failure to do so will result in denial of the claim." PS Form 3831.
Appeals: first appeal "within 30 days of the date of the original decision"; final appeal to the Consumer Advocate "within 30 days of the date of the second denial".
Insurance: "If insurance is not purchased at the time of mailing, the United States Postal Service® is unable to honor any requests to be compensated for lost, missing, and/or damaged item(s)." "Claims without a mailing receipt can be filed, but payment may be limited" (caps by service: not used). Special cases: airline tickets (only the price of filing a Lost Ticket Report), gift cards and similar items not paid, money (a separate lower limit; Registered Mail excepted), checks not considered cash, collectibles by receipt or statement of value. Eligible items: Priority Mail and USPS Ground Advantage include up to $100 (merchandise only) — amount not used in the article.
"For more details on payable claims, see the Domestic Mail Manual 609."

## S3 — USPS, Domestic Mail Manual 609 (primary, official)

URL https://pe.usps.com/text/dmm300/609.htm. No date shown on the page.
1.3 Who May File: either the mailer or addressee for damaged articles or articles with contents missing; either the mailer or addressee "who is in possession of the original retail mailing receipt, or in possession of the online label record or computer printout of the Web-based application" for lost articles; only the account holder for insured USPS Returns packages (with conditions); only the mailer when the mailer added and paid for insurance on USPS Returns packages; only the mailer for insured or COD parcels paid using USPS Ship.
1.4 When to File: same two cases; chart has "Insured Mail (including Priority Mail under 503.4.2) 15 days 60 days"; **no separate domestic Ground Advantage row** (tested: no "usps ground advantage 15 days 60 days", no "priority mail 15 days 60 days"; "USPS Ground Advantage — Retail" appears only in the APO/FPO/DPO row).
1.5: online preferred, proof of value as .pdf or .jpeg; by mail Form 1000; "For pieces with multiple extra services, the customer must provide original receipts for all services purchased."
2.0: addressee must keep container, articles, packaging, contents; "Failure to do so will result in denial of the claim."
3.1 Evidence of Insurance: original mailing receipt; "For insured and COD mail, a photocopy of the original mailing receipt is acceptable."; outer packaging ("If only the outer packaging is submitted, indemnity can be limited" — caps by service, not used); for insurance bought online, the printout "must identify the USPS Tracking number of the insured parcel, total postage paid, insurance fee paid, declared value, mailing date, origin ZIP Code, and delivery ZIP Code", and "either a postmarked (round-dated) original mailing receipt (Form 3877 firm sheet), or an electronic shipment acceptance scan ... followed by a valid acceptance scan event or a physical scan event must also be available as evidence of mailing, in addition to the online record."
4.3 Nonpayable Claims (examples used): evidence of insurance coverage not provided; loss, damage or missing contents that occurred after delivery by the USPS; claim based solely on sentimental rather than actual value; perishable contents frozen, melted, spoiled or deteriorated; damage by abrasion, scarring or scraping to articles not properly wrapped for protection; fragile nature of article prevented its safe carriage in the mail, regardless of packaging.

## S4 — USPS.com, "Shipping Insurance and Delivery Services" (one line each)

URL https://www.usps.com/ship/insurance-extra-services.htm. Priority Mail and USPS Ground Advantage include insurance when the domestic shipment "must have an applicable USPS Tracking barcode" (amount not used). "When your mail item is lost, you should file a claim no later than 60 days from the date of mailing. If your item is damaged or missing contents, you should file a claim immediately but not later than 60 days from the date of mailing." No opening day given.

## Differences shown in the article (not resolved)

1. Receipt: "must have the original mailing receipt" (S1) against photocopy accepted for insured and COD mail (S3) and a claim without a receipt can be filed but payment may be limited (S2).
2. Online label record alone (S1 lists it as evidence of insurance) against required fields and an evidence of mailing (S3).
3. Domestic Priority Mail lumped with Insured Mail and no domestic Ground Advantage row (S3) against separate rows (S1, S2).
4. Appeal clock: from receiving the decision (S1) against from the date of the decision (S2).
5. Opening day for lost items: given (S1, S2, S3); not given on the insurance page (S4).
6. Saved claims deleted after 3 days (S2) against no period (S1).

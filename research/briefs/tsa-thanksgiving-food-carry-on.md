## Topic

Which Thanksgiving foods TSA says can go through a checkpoint in a carry-on and which should be in checked baggage.

## Working title (proposal)

Can You Bring Thanksgiving Food Through TSA?

## Search intent and focus keyword

- **Proposed focus keyword:** `can you bring thanksgiving food through tsa`
- **Intent:** practical rule question; peaks around the holiday (Claude's judgment, not measured).
- **Demand signal:** not checked by autocomplete (tool unreliable on 2026-10-07). A web search for the question on 2026-10-07 returned mostly news, travel and food-site pages and several older TSA releases (titles only). **Search volume and competition were not checked.**

## Audience

US travelers carrying holiday food on a plane.

## Angle

1. **The rule in one sentence (TSA Nov 2025 release).** Most foods can go through a TSA checkpoint, but items that can "spill, spread, spray, pump, or pour" and are larger than 3.4 ounces should be packed in checked baggage.
2. **TSA's carry-on examples (release).** Pies, cakes, cookies and brownies; turkey, chicken, ham and steak; stuffing; solid casseroles; cooked mac and cheese; fresh vegetables; fresh fruit including cranberries; candy; spices.
3. **TSA's checked-baggage examples (release).** Cranberry sauce (homemade or canned, "as it is spreadable"); gravy (homemade or in a jar or can); canned fruits or vegetables in liquid-filled cans or jars; preserves, jams and jellies; maple syrup. A casserole with liquid or semi-liquid components must follow the 3-1-1 liquids rule.
4. **Why (TSA Food page).** "Solid food items (not liquids or gels) can be transported in either your carry-on or checked bags." "Liquid or gel food items larger than 3.4 oz are not allowed in carry-on bags". And: "The final decision rests with the TSA officer on whether an item is allowed through the checkpoint."

## Why now

Thanksgiving is November 26 (OPM), 50 days away. See "Timing".

## Scope: in and out

- **In:** the solid-versus-liquid distinction as TSA states it, the examples above attributed to TSA with the release date, the officer's final-decision statement, a pointer to the published 3-1-1 article.
- **Out:** airline or destination rules, international food import rules, frozen or dry-ice items (not read), alcohol, food-safety or health advice (the release has a food-safety line; leave it out), recipes, advice on how to pack.

## Official source notes (read 2026-10-07 in the built-in browser)

- **TSA, "What Can I Bring? — Food"**, https://www.tsa.gov/travel/security-screening/whatcanibring/food . Bread, Candy: "Solid food items (not liquids or gels) can be transported in either your carry-on or checked bags. Liquid or gel food items larger than 3.4 oz are not allowed in carry-on bags and should be placed in your checked bags if possible." Canned foods: carry-on "Yes (Special Instructions)"; "they could require additional screening that might result in the item not being allowed through the checkpoint." Footer: "The final decision rests with the TSA officer on whether an item is allowed through the checkpoint." The page lists 59 items; only the first page (11 items) was read in this pass. No update date shown.
- **TSA press release, "TSA prepared for busy Thanksgiving travel in Harrisburg, offers special packing tips"**, Monday, November 24, 2025, https://www.tsa.gov/news/press/releases/2025/11/24/tsa-prepared-for-busy-thanksgiving-travel-harrisburg-offers-special . Lists the carry-on and checked examples as above, and "Most foods can be carried through a TSA checkpoint, but some items must be transported in checked baggage." Similar local releases exist for other airports; one release is enough as the source, and the national 2022 release (https://www.tsa.gov/news/press/releases/2022/11/16/tsa-dishes-what-thanksgiving-foods-can-be-carried-through-security) was not read.

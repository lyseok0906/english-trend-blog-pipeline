## Scope decision: what one article can say accurately

**In (each attributed to the USPS page that says it):**

1. **What it is:** a numbered Extra Service bought at the time of mailing, with a mailing receipt (Form 3800 / Receipt for Certified Mail) and, if requested, electronic verification that an item was delivered or that a delivery attempt was made (S1, S2, S3).
2. **How the status is checked:** online at usps.com by the extra service number on the receipt, by phone (1-800-222-1811 on the DMM page), or by bulk file transfer for mailers with an electronic manifest (S1, S2).
3. **What it can be combined with:** Return Receipt (hardcopy Form 3811 or electronic); Restricted Delivery (named individual or authorized agent); Adult Signature Required and Adult Signature Restricted Delivery stated as "not available at retail" and mentioned in one line (S1, S2).
4. **Which mail it goes with:** Priority Mail and First-Class Mail; travels at the speed of that class; not available with international mail; may go to APO/FPO/DPO and U.S. territories (S1, S2).
5. **What it does not include:** no insurance of its own (Priority Mail's included insurance is kept); insurance cannot be bought as an extra service alongside it (S1, S2).
6. **What happens if no one is home:** held at the Post Office 15 days, returned on day 16, per the FAQ only (S1).
7. **Refunds:** the FAQ's four conditions and the 30-to-60-day window; "at the discretion of the local Post Office" (S1).
8. **Fees:** either one sentence pointing to Notice 123 (no number), or a dated price (user decision).

**Out of scope (named in the draft's scope line):**

- Legal effect, legal notice, court or agency requirements, evidence value, state rules. Whether a business, landlord or agency must use it.
- Certified Mail versus Registered Mail, Signature Confirmation and Certificate of Mailing as a comparison (USPS lists them as separate services; a comparison needs more sources and more claims; possible later post).
- Step-by-step filling of Form 3800 (the article states the green label and where the sender enters the recipient's address, no instructions beyond what USPS pages say), online ordering via third-party mailing services, Click-N-Ship, bulk and commercial mailing (firm sheets).
- International mail, prices of other services, delivery times.

## Source conflicts and limits, and how the article handles them

1. **Signature wording.** The FAQ says Certified Mail "Requires a signature from the addressee"; the DMM says USPS keeps a delivery record that includes the signature, and the USPS.com page says the signature is available "when combined with Return Receipt". The article attributes each wording to its page and does not say how a reader can get a signature without a return receipt.
2. **"Prove you sent it" (USPS.com wording).** Quoted at most once and attributed as USPS's wording, with the statement that this article does not discuss what it means legally.
3. **15-day hold.** Appears in the FAQ only among the pages read; attributed to the FAQ.
4. **Adult Signature services.** The FAQ says not available at retail; the DMM exhibit footnote "(PM only)" could not be resolved from the page text. One line, attributed to the FAQ, or leave out.
5. **Fee currency.** Notice 123 states "Effective October 04, 2026"; whether another change is scheduled is not known.

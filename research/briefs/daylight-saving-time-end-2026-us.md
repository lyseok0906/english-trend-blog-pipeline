## Topic

When daylight saving time ends in the United States in 2026, what happens to the clocks, which places do not change, and who sets the rule.

## Working title (proposal)

When Does Daylight Saving Time End in 2026? What Changes and Where It Doesn't Apply

## Audience

English-speaking readers, primarily in the US, who want a plain answer and want to know whether their area is affected.

## Angle (what the article adds beyond a date)

1. **The date and the moment.** Ends Sunday, November 1, 2026 at 2:00 a.m. local time; clocks go back to 1:00 a.m., so one hour repeats.
2. **Where it does not apply.** Hawaii, American Samoa, Guam, Puerto Rico, the U.S. Virgin Islands, and Arizona (except the Navajo Indian Reservation, as NIST words it). A table, not a map.
3. **Who decides.** The rule (second Sunday of March to first Sunday of November) comes from federal law; the Department of Transportation, not NIST, regulates time zones and daylight saving time; a state may exempt itself by law to stay on standard time. This explains why some places never change.

The answer (date, time, one-sentence rule) goes in the first screen. The article is one answer to one intent; state names that appear in autocomplete variants are not separate articles.

## Scope: in and out

- **In:** current US rule and the 2026 dates as stated by NIST and the statute; the list of places that do not observe daylight saving time; who regulates it.
- **Out of scope (decision from `docs/blog-c-lanes-planning.md`):** legislation about ending or making daylight saving time permanent. It changes often and is political. The article states only what current law and NIST say, each with an as-of date, and does not claim anything about pending proposals. This leaves the "is it ending" readers with a short answer about current law only; whether that is enough is for the user and reviewers to judge.
- **Out of scope:** health, sleep or safety advice; other countries (Canada, UK, EU and Australia change on different dates, and no source was read for them); city- or state-by-state clock times.
- Do not recommend actions. Explain what the sources say.

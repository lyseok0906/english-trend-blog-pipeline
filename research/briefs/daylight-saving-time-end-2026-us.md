## Audience

## Angle (what the article adds beyond a date)

1. **The date and the moment.** Ends Sunday, November 1, 2026 at 2:00 a.m. local time; clocks go back to 1:00 a.m., so one hour repeats (NIST, statute).
2. **Where it does not apply.** The Department of Transportation lists Hawaii, American Samoa, Guam, the Northern Mariana Islands, Puerto Rico, the Virgin Islands and most of Arizona. NIST describes Arizona as the exception to the Navajo Indian Reservation, and its own list omits the Northern Mariana Islands. The draft attributes the list to DOT and says which source says what. A table, not a map.
3. **Who decides.** The rule (second Sunday of March to first Sunday of November) is in federal law (15 U.S.C. 260a, amended in 2005). The Department of Transportation oversees time zones and uniform daylight saving time; a state may exempt itself by law to stay on standard time. DOT says it does not have the power to repeal or change daylight saving time.

The answer (date, time, one-sentence rule) goes in the first screen. The article answers one intent; state names that appear in autocomplete variants are not separate articles.

## Scope: in and out

- **In:** the current US rule and the 2026 dates as stated by NIST, DOT and the statute; the list of places that do not observe daylight saving time; who regulates it; the DOT sentence on what DOT can and cannot change.
- **Out of scope (decision from `docs/blog-c-lanes-planning.md`):** legislation about ending or making daylight saving time permanent. It changes often and is political. The article states what the sources say about current law and does not claim anything about pending proposals. "Is it ending this year" readers get the current rule and DOT's statement, nothing about proposals.
- **Out of scope:** health, sleep or safety advice; other countries (Canada, UK, EU and Australia change on different dates, and no source was read for them); city- or state-by-state clock times; states' ability to adopt permanent daylight saving time beyond the one DOT statement, until its wording is confirmed (source note S3-3).
- Do not recommend actions. Explain what the sources say.

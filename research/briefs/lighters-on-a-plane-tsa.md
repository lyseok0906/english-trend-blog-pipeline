## Scope decision: what one article can say accurately

The answer depends on the lighter type and on which agency's page is read, so the article is built on four types and one adjacent item, and stops there.

**In (each with its own row, each attributed to the page that says it):**

1. **Disposable (butane) and Zippo-type (absorbed liquid) lighters.** TSA: carry-on Yes; checked bags "Yes (Special Instructions)" (without fuel allowed; with fuel prohibited unless the DOT exemption of up to two fueled lighters in a DOT approved case applies). FAA page and chart: one lighter per passenger in carry-on or on one's person; chart row says "Not allowed in Checked Bags". 49 CFR 175.10(a)(2): "a lighter intended for use by an individual when carried on one's person or in carry-on baggage only".
2. **Arc, electronic and other lithium-battery lighters.** TSA (arc/electronic page, Nov 4, 2024): carry-on Yes with special instructions, checked No; prevent unintentional activation; battery limits 100 Wh (lithium ion) or 2 g (lithium metal); no recharging on board. TSA lithium page (Oct 10, 2024): carry-on Yes, checked No. FAA page: carry-on only, same measures and limits. Regulation adds a safety cap and the UN 38.3 test requirement.
3. **Torch lighters.** TSA: No / No. FAA page and chart: not allowed in the cabin or in checked baggage.
4. **Lighters using unabsorbed liquid fuel (desk/table, antique wick lighters).** FAA page: forbidden; regulation: not permitted on one's person, in carry-on or in checked baggage. (FAA is the only page read for this type; TSA has no separate page in the five read.)
5. **Lighter fluid and refills (adjacent item, one row).** TSA lighter fluid page: No / No (Oct 10, 2024); regulation: lighter fuel and refills not permitted anywhere.

**Out of scope (named in the draft's scope line where useful):**

- The DOT special-permit travel containers (brands, permit numbers, how they work). The only permit document found (DOT-SP 14415, third revision, April 26, 2013) shows an expiration date of February 28, 2017, and no current permit was verified, so the article does not explain the mechanism. It reports only what TSA's page says about the exemption, attributed and dated.
- Matches (the FAA chart has a row; the TSA matches page was not read), e-cigarettes and vapes, camping stoves, butane canisters, chef/micro/utility torches (these appear only in the 2013 FAQ), lighters shaped like weapons (2013 FAQ only), mailing lighters (2013 FAQ only).
- Other countries' rules and airline policies (not read). Smoking rules, state or local laws.

## The source conflicts, and how the article handles them

1. **Checked bags, disposable and Zippo-type.** TSA's page says "Yes (Special Instructions)" with a DOT exemption for up to two fueled lighters in an approved case. The FAA web page and chart say lighters are for carry-on or on the person, and the chart row says not allowed in checked bags. The regulation text allows a lighter "on one's person or in carry-on baggage only". The article reports both statements, labels each, and does not merge them. The practical line it can support from the sources: the carry-on or pocket is where every page agrees a lighter can go.
2. **How many lighters.** The FAA page and the 2013 FAQ say one per passenger; the regulation says "a lighter" (singular). The TSA disposable/Zippo page states no number. The article attributes the "one" to the FAA and the regulation and says TSA's page does not state a number.
3. **The 2013 FAA FAQ (opened directly, 2 pages).** It says torch lighters in DOT special-permit containers may go in checked baggage (domestic travel only), which contradicts the TSA torch page (No / No) and the FAA's current web page and chart (not allowed in cabin or checked baggage). It is older (November 18, 2013) than both. Proposed handling: follow the current pages, and mention the FAQ at most in one dated sentence in the "where the pages differ" part, or leave it out of the body and keep it only in the source notes. **User/ChatGPT decision requested.**

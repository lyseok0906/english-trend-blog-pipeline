## Topic

What TSA says TSA PreCheck is, what it lets a traveler leave in the bag, and what it does not guarantee.

## Working title (proposal)

What Is TSA PreCheck? What It Changes at the Checkpoint

## Search intent and focus keyword

- **Proposed focus keyword:** `what is tsa precheck`
- **Intent:** informational. Many results are promotional; the article is neutral and does not recommend enrolling.
- **Demand signal:** not checked by autocomplete (tool unreliable on 2026-10-07). **Search volume and competition were not checked.** Competition from travel and credit-card sites is likely (Claude's judgment, not checked).

## Audience

US travelers who have heard of PreCheck and want a plain description of what it changes.

## Angle

1. **What it is (TSA).** TSA PreCheck "gives trusted travelers a speedier security experience in dedicated lanes across the U.S."
2. **What changes (TSA).** "With TSA PreCheck leave in your bag electronics and 3-1-1 liquids and leave on belts, light jackets, and shoes." (This links to the published TSA 3-1-1 article.)
3. **What it does not do (TSA).** "All travelers will be screened, and no individual is guaranteed expedited screening."
4. **How the airline side works (TSA).** A Known Traveler Number (KTN) is entered in airline reservations and travel profiles to get TSA PreCheck on the boarding pass; children 17 and under can join an adult in the lane; TSA lists three authorized enrollment providers (CLEAR, IDEMIA, Telos) and says the benefits last five years.

## Why now

No deadline. Holiday travel interest may rise from November (Claude's judgment). Not time-bound.

## Scope: in and out

- **In:** the four points above, attributed to TSA with the as-of date.
- **Out:** prices and promotions (the page lists several provider prices and a CLEAR+ offer), whether enrolling is worth it, comparisons with Global Entry or CLEAR, wait-time claims unless attributed (the page says about 99% of passengers wait less than 10 minutes; that is TSA's own statement and the article would attribute it, but it reads as promotional, see open question 2), the standard-lane shoe rule (see Risks), eligibility rules and enrollment steps.
- The article describes; it does not recommend actions.

## Risks

- Promotional page: price and offer text is commercial; the article must leave it out and stay descriptive, or it reads like an ad.
- The page says PreCheck members "leave on ... shoes", while a TSA/DHS release of July 8, 2025 (https://www.tsa.gov/news/press/releases/2025/07/08/dhs-end-shoes-travel-policy) announced that all passengers in domestic airports may keep shoes on. Whether the current standard-lane practice is stated on an evergreen TSA page was not verified; the article should attribute only what the PreCheck page says and not describe the standard lane.
- Only one TSA page has been read.

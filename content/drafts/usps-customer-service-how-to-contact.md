# USPS Customer Service: How to Phone, Email or File a Complaint

**USPS pages list one main customer line, 1-800-ASK-USPS (1-800-275-8777), plus separate phone lines for tracking and for technical problems, and an Email Us form that sends you to a different place depending on the reason for your inquiry.** For complaints, a government page adds routes outside USPS customer service for employee misconduct, mail fraud and postal policy. This article puts those routes side by side and groups them by the problem each one is for.

This article uses the USPS.com "Contact Us" page (no update date shown), the USPS "Email Us" page at emailus.usps.com (no update date shown) and the USAGov page "How to file a U.S. Postal Service complaint" (last updated September 4, 2026), all read on November 26, 2026. Phone numbers and hours are copied as the pages list them on that date and can change.

## Start with the FAQs and tracking

The Contact Us page says the most common questions are about tracking packages or delayed delivery and points first to the USPS FAQs, including "USPS Tracking - The Basics," "Tracking Status Notifications" and "Missing Mail - The Basics." It also notes that the tracking information available to customer service representatives is the same information you see on USPS Tracking, and that you can call if you want to talk to someone about another issue with your package. Our articles [USPS Tracking Status: What Each One Means and What USPS Says to Do](https://statesideexplained.com/usps-tracking-status-what-it-means/) and [USPS Missing Mail Search: When You Can File and What You Need](https://statesideexplained.com/usps-missing-mail-search-how-it-works/) cover those two topics.

## The phone lines

The Contact Us page lists three phone lines with the same hours: Monday to Friday 8 AM to 8:30 PM Eastern Time and Saturday 8 AM to 6 PM Eastern Time.

- **Customer Care Center: 1-800-ASK-USPS (1-800-275-8777).** The page describes it as the way to connect with customer service representatives to resolve an issue.
- **Domestic and international USPS Tracking: 1-800-222-1811.** This is the number the page gives for speaking with a representative about a package.
- **Technical Support: 1-800-344-7779.** The page says the team can help when a website application or form does not work properly, and names Click-N-Ship, File a Claim, PO Boxes, The Postal Store and USPS accounts.

The page also lists separate numbers for stamps and Postal Store orders and for free shipping supplies, with their own hours; this article does not cover them. For people who are deaf, hard of hearing, deaf-blind or have speech impairments, the page says to use the FCC's Telecommunications Relay Services (TRS) by dialing 7-1-1 or by looking up your state on the FCC's page. It adds that the TRS contact for your state is not a USPS customer service representative and cannot handle USPS-specific issues.

## Email Us: the topic decides where you go

The Email Us page opens with the question "What's the reason for your inquiry?" and says it asks you to pick a topic so that USPS can direct you to the correct place. It lists six topics, each with examples:

- **Where is my package?** A missing package or an envelope with a tracking number, with Priority Mail, Priority Mail Express and Certified Mail packages as examples.
- **Where is my mail?** Mail services, with examples such as mail not delivered daily as expected, Redelivery, Change of Address, Package Pickup Services, Premium Forwarding Services, theft of mail and Hold Mail.
- **USPS.com.** A technical issue with products or services on USPS.com, with Click-N-Ship, PO Box and Informed Delivery as examples.
- **Personnel.** A compliment, behavior or professionalism concerning a postal employee.
- **Postal Facility.** Access, signage, hours or appearance of a USPS location.
- **Permits and Bulk Mail.** Permit holders, non-profit and bulk mail.

The Contact Us page adds three online routes of its own: an online form to describe your issue, a chat feature for technical issues with USPS.com services, and a separate email address for Informed Delivery user support.

## Complaints and where else to go

The USAGov page says you can complain about mail service online, by phone or in person. Online means choosing a topic on the Email Us page, which it says includes delivery issues and complaints about USPS employees or facilities. By phone means 1-800-ASK-USPS (TTY: 711). In person means speaking with the station manager at your local Post Office. If the complaint is not resolved, it says you can find your USPS Consumer Affairs office or write to the Office of the Consumer Advocate at the address it gives.

The same page names three other places:

- **USPS Office of the Inspector General (OIG).** For theft, fraud or waste at a USPS facility or by a USPS employee, with an online complaint and a phone line.
- **U.S. Postal Inspection Service (USPIS).** The federal law enforcement agency that protects the mail system, for mail fraud and mail theft by a person or company outside USPS, filed online.
- **Postal Regulatory Commission (PRC).** For a comment or complaint about a policy change such as postage rates, through its online contact form.

## Which route fits which problem

The pages describe these routes for different situations rather than as one ordered list, so this section groups them by problem.

- **A delivery or mail service problem.** The Email Us page offers "Where is my package?" and "Where is my mail?" as topics. The USAGov page adds the main phone number and the station manager at your local Post Office, and, if the complaint is not resolved, USPS Consumer Affairs and the Office of the Consumer Advocate.
- **A question about a tracked package.** The Contact Us page gives a separate tracking phone line and says representatives see the same tracking information you do. The Email Us page has the "Where is my package?" topic for a package or envelope with a tracking number.
- **A technical problem on USPS.com.** The Contact Us page lists Technical Support by phone and a chat feature. The Email Us page has a USPS.com topic that includes Informed Delivery, and the Contact Us page also lists a separate email address for Informed Delivery user support.
- **Mail theft or fraud.** The Email Us page lists "Theft of Mail" as an example under "Where is my mail?" The USAGov page names the Office of the Inspector General for theft, fraud or waste at a USPS facility or by a USPS employee, and the Postal Inspection Service for mail fraud or theft by a person or company outside USPS.
- **A policy change such as postage rates.** The USAGov page names the Postal Regulatory Commission.
- **Hearing or speech needs.** The USAGov page lists "TTY: 711" beside the main number. The Contact Us page describes the FCC's Telecommunications Relay Services, reached by dialing 7-1-1, and says that contact is not a USPS customer service representative.

## What the pages do not say

They do not say which route to start with if you are unsure who took a piece of mail, how long an email reply takes, whether a case or reference number is given, or how long a phone wait is. They do not list holiday hours for the three phone lines above; the only closure stated is for the stamps and Postal Store line, which the page says is closed on Saturdays, Sundays and Postal Holidays. They also do not say what to have ready before you call.

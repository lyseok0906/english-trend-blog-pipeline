# USPS General Delivery: How to Receive Mail Without a Permanent Address

**USPS pages describe General Delivery as a free service in which mail addressed to you is held at an authorized Post Office for you to pick up in person, mainly as a temporary address for people without a permanent one.** The FAQ says to find the authorized facility, contact it to confirm the service and the ZIP Code, and address the mail as NAME, GENERAL DELIVERY, CITY STATE ZIP. Both pages say a postmaster can restrict the service if a customer cannot present suitable identification, and both say each piece is held for no more than 30 days, a limit the FAQ words with more detail than the Domestic Mail Manual (DMM).

This article uses the USPS FAQ "What is General Delivery?" (Article 000006989, dated August 4, 2026) and section 6.0 of DMM 508, "Recipient Services" (no update date shown), with section 11.1 of the same DMM chapter for Hold Mail. All were read on November 24, 2026. It gives no fees or prices; the FAQ says the service is free where it is authorized.

## What General Delivery is

The FAQ says General Delivery is a USPS service where mail for you is held at a local Post Office to pick up in person, and that it is often used to give a temporary mailing address to people without a permanent address. It adds that the service is also intended for these cases:

- Post Office locations without city carrier delivery service.
- Non-city delivery offices, for people who prefer not to use Post Office Box service and for whom a Post Office box, Caller Service or delivery by letter carrier would be an unreasonable inconvenience.
- A participating Post Office serving transients (people who travel extensively) and people without a permanent address.
- Anyone who wants Post Office box service when boxes are unavailable.

DMM 508.6.1 gives a shorter statement of purpose: General Delivery is intended primarily as a temporary means of delivery, for transients and customers not permanently located and for customers who want Post Office box service when boxes are unavailable.

## Finding the Post Office and writing the address

The FAQ says that before you give anyone a General Delivery address, you should find which local postal facility is authorized to accept General Delivery mail by using Find USPS Locations and filtering for "General Delivery," and then contact that facility to confirm the service is available, to confirm the ZIP Code for the address and to ask any other questions. DMM 508.6.3 gives the same advice to customers before mailing: contact the destination Post Office to find the authorized facility or facilities and their ZIP Codes.

The FAQ gives the format for the mail as three lines: NAME, then GENERAL DELIVERY, then CITY STATE ZIP. Both pages say General Delivery is normally available at only one facility under a Post Office that runs several, that a postmaster may authorize more than one according to customer and operational needs, and that a customer may use only one such location.

## Identification and limits

Both pages say a postmaster can limit the service for two reasons: the customer cannot present suitable identification, or the customer's mail volume or service level (the pages give mail accumulation as an example) cannot reasonably be accommodated. The FAQ says a postmaster may restrict the use; the DMM says a postmaster may refuse or restrict it. DMM 508.6.3 adds that a General Delivery customer can be required to present suitable identification before mail is given to the customer. The FAQ sends further questions on restrictions to the General Delivery facility.

The FAQ also says:

- General Delivery is a free service when and where it is authorized, and no application is required.
- People interested in General Delivery should speak with the postmaster, and any limit on how long you can use General Delivery is set by the postmaster.
- General Delivery mail is not delivered on Sunday or holidays.
- A person who wants to pick up mail before regular delivery occurs is advised to buy a Post Office Box for what the FAQ calls a nominal fee, so that mail is available every morning. The FAQ gives no amount, and neither does this article.

## How long mail is held

| | FAQ | DMM 508.6.4 |
|---|---|---|
| Longest hold | Each piece is held for no more than 30 days, unless the sender asks for a shorter period | Each piece is held for no more than 30 days, although the sender may ask for a shorter period |
| Longer hold | May be held longer if the sender or addressee asks and the postmaster approves | Not mentioned |
| Mail without a specific address or sender instructions | Held 10 days at an office with letter-carrier service, 15 days at an office without | Not mentioned |

The FAQ puts the 10-day and 15-day periods in a note after the 30-day rule, and the DMM section has neither the note nor the longer-hold sentence. The pages do not say how the three rules fit together for a particular item, so ask the General Delivery facility about the item rather than counting on 30 days.

## General Delivery, PO Box and Hold Mail

A PO Box is a separate service with its own application and availability rules, covered in our article [How Does a USPS PO Box Work? Sizes, ID and Service Periods](https://statesideexplained.com/usps-po-box-how-it-works/), which reports that the DMM lets a postmaster provide general delivery until a box is available. The FAQ and DMM 508.6.1 both name the same situation: a customer who wants a box when boxes are unavailable.

USPS Hold Mail is a different service. DMM 508.11.1 says it lets a customer, or an authorized agent, have all mail for an eligible address held at the Post Office for not less than 3 days and not more than 30 days. It applies to an eligible address; General Delivery is mail addressed to a person at the Post Office. Our article [How Does USPS Hold Mail Work? Limits and Steps](https://statesideexplained.com/usps-hold-mail-how-it-works/) covers that service. The pages read for this article do not say how a General Delivery request and a Hold Mail request would interact.

## Where the USPS pages differ

- **How long mail is held.** The FAQ allows a longer hold if the sender or addressee asks and the postmaster approves; the DMM section states only the 30-day maximum.
- **Mail with no specific address or instructions.** The FAQ gives 10 and 15 days; the DMM section gives no such period. The FAQ does not define "a specific address" in that note.
- **Restricting or refusing.** The FAQ says a postmaster may restrict use; the DMM says a postmaster may refuse or restrict it.
- **Purposes.** The FAQ lists four situations, including Post Office locations without city carrier delivery; the DMM lists two.

## What the pages do not say

They do not say which kinds of identification count as suitable, or what the hours for pick-up are; the FAQ points to the facility for both questions. They do not say whether someone else can collect your mail, whether the service takes packages or only letters (both pages say "mail"), or what happens to an item at the end of its hold. They do not say how a sender asks for a shorter period. For a particular facility, the answer is whatever that Post Office tells you.

# USPS General Delivery: How to Receive Mail Without a Permanent Address

USPS General Delivery lets a recipient collect mail addressed to General Delivery at an authorized Post Office. It is intended mainly as a temporary way to receive mail, including for people without a permanent location or people waiting for a PO Box. Before giving the address to a sender, contact the destination Post Office to confirm which facility offers the service and which ZIP Code to use. USPS may require suitable identification.

This article describes USPS's General Delivery FAQ and Domestic Mail Manual (DMM) section 508.6.0, read November 24, 2026. The FAQ describes additional situations in which the service can be used; the DMM describes its primary purpose and restrictions.

## Where can you receive General Delivery mail?

The DMM says General Delivery is normally available at one facility under a Post Office that administers multiple facilities. A postmaster may authorize more than one, but a customer may use only one such location.

The USPS FAQ advises using Find USPS Locations with the General Delivery filter, then contacting the facility to confirm availability and the ZIP Code. Hours and local arrangements should be checked with that facility before a sender addresses the mail.

## How should the address be written?

The FAQ gives this format:

```text
NAME
GENERAL DELIVERY
CITY STATE ZIP
```

Use the city, state and ZIP Code confirmed by the destination Post Office. The DMM separately tells customers to contact the destination Post Office before mailing to determine the authorized facility and applicable ZIP Code.

## Who can use it, and is identification required?

The DMM describes General Delivery primarily as temporary delivery for transients and people not permanently located, and for customers who want a PO Box when boxes are unavailable. The FAQ lists other circumstances, including some locations without city carrier delivery.

A postmaster may restrict or refuse the service if the customer cannot present suitable identification, or if the customer's mail volume or accumulation cannot reasonably be accommodated. The DMM says a customer can be required to show suitable identification before mail is handed over. Ask the destination office what identification it accepts for your situation.

The FAQ says the service is free where authorized and no application is required. It says any limit on how long a person may use the service is determined by the postmaster. Those statements concern access to the service; they do not mean each individual mailpiece can remain indefinitely.

## How long will USPS hold the mail?

The DMM says each General Delivery mailpiece is held for no more than **30 days**, although the sender may request a shorter period. The FAQ also describes a longer holding period if requested by the sender or addressee and approved by the postmaster; the DMM passage does not mention that extension. The FAQ separately gives 10- and 15-day periods for mail without a specific address or sender instructions, depending on whether the office has letter-carrier service. Ask the destination office about the particular item rather than treating 30 days as guaranteed.

## Is it the same as a PO Box or Hold Mail?

General Delivery uses a Post Office authorized for that service. A PO Box is a separate service with its own application and availability rules. See [USPS PO Box: How It Works](https://statesideexplained.com/usps-po-box-how-it-works/) for the box service. The DMM describes USPS Hold Mail as holding mail for an eligible address for a requested period; it has its own rules. General Delivery should not be assumed to change an existing Hold Mail request.

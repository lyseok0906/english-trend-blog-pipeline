# Can You Bring a Razor on a Plane? What TSA Says About Each Type

**It depends on the type of razor.** TSA's pages list disposable razors and electric razors as allowed in both carry-on and checked bags. For a safety razor, TSA says it is allowed through the checkpoint without the blade, and for razor blades that are not in a cartridge, TSA says they are prohibited in carry-on bags and allowed in checked bags.

This article covers what four TSA "What Can I Bring?" pages say: Disposable Razor, Safety Razor With Blades (allowed without blade), Razor-Type Blades and Electric Razors. The wording is as of November 9, 2026. Three of the four pages carry dates from 2017 or 2023, and the article gives each date. It does not cover straight razors, shaving cream, other sharp objects, airline rules or other countries.

## The four pages in one table

| Type of razor | TSA: carry-on bags | TSA: checked bags | What the page adds | Page date |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Disposable razor | Yes | Yes | Sharp objects in checked bags should be sheathed or securely wrapped | May 4, 2017 |
| Safety razor with blades (allowed without blade) | No (header line) | Yes | Allowed through the checkpoint without the blade; the blade must be removed before the checkpoint; officers are not authorized to remove it | June 22, 2023 |
| Razor-type blades | No | Yes | Box cutters and razor blades not in a cartridge are prohibited in carry-on | May 4, 2017 |
| Electric razors | Yes | Yes | "If you have questions regarding regulations, please see the FAA website." | July 14, 2026 |

## Disposable razors

TSA's Disposable Razor page lists carry-on bags "Yes" and checked bags "Yes." Its only other instruction is the line TSA puts on its sharp-object pages for checked bags: any sharp objects in checked bags should be sheathed or securely wrapped to prevent injury to baggage handlers and inspectors. The page does not mention cartridges or a limit on how many razors you can carry.

## Safety razors

TSA's page for a safety razor with blades is titled "Safety Razor With Blades (allowed without blade)." Its header line says "Carry On Bags: No" and "Checked Bags: Yes." Its text says a safety razor is allowed through the screening checkpoint without the blade, that the blade must be removed before entering the checkpoint, and that TSA officers are not authorized to remove the blades from the holder. So, as TSA writes it, the razor can go through the checkpoint when the blade is already out. The page says no more about a razor that still has its blade at the checkpoint than that officers are not authorized to take the blade out. The page also repeats the line about wrapping sharp objects in checked baggage.

## Loose razor blades

TSA's Razor-Type Blades page lists carry-on bags "No" and checked bags "Yes." It says box cutters and razor blades not in a cartridge are prohibited in carry-on. The page names only blades that are not in a cartridge; it says nothing about blades that are.

## Electric razors

TSA's Electric Razors page lists carry-on bags "Yes" and checked bags "Yes" and adds one sentence: "If you have questions regarding regulations, please see the FAA website." It is the only one of the four pages that shows a 2026 date (July 14, 2026). The page does not mention batteries. This article does not read any FAA page, so it says nothing about battery rules for electric razors.

## Packing razors in a checked bag

The pages for disposable razors, safety razors and razor-type blades all say that any sharp objects in checked bags (the safety razor page says "checked baggage") should be sheathed or securely wrapped to prevent injury to baggage handlers and inspectors. The electric razor page does not include that line.

## What the pages do not say

None of the four pages mentions straight razors. A search for "razor" in TSA's What Can I Bring? item list returns exactly these four pages and none about straight razors, so the article says nothing about them. None of the pages gives a limit on the number of razors or blades. TSA says on each page that the final decision rests with the TSA officer on whether an item is allowed through the checkpoint. Shaving cream is a different item with its own TSA page and is a liquids and aerosols question, covered in [What Is the TSA 3-1-1 Liquids Rule? What's Exempt?](https://statesideexplained.com/tsa-3-1-1-liquids-rule/).

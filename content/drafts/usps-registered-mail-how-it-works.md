# USPS Registered Mail: Security, Tracking and Delivery Explained

**USPS describes Registered Mail as its most secure mail service: the FAQ says it is protected by safes, cages, sealed containers, locks and keys, it requires a signature on delivery, and it must be presented to a retail employee or a rural carrier.** The FAQ also says Registered Mail alone cannot be tracked through the mailstream: it gives delivery information when a scan shows a delivery or an attempted delivery, not scans from facility to facility. A system of receipts monitors the item from acceptance to delivery, but the FAQ says those receipts are not readily available and can be reached only by starting a claim form.

This article covers domestic Registered Mail. It uses the USPS FAQ "Registered Mail - The Basics" (Article 000006061, dated May 14, 2025) and section 2.0 of Domestic Mail Manual (DMM) 503, "Additional Mailing Services" (no update date shown), and one table row in the FAQ "Missing Mail - The Basics" (Article 000007968, dated May 19, 2026), all read on November 24, 2026. The first two pages give dollar limits for insurance and for cash shipments; this article gives no prices, fees or insurance amounts. It does not compare Registered Mail with Certified Mail, which has its own article: [What Is USPS Certified Mail? What It Includes and How It Works](https://statesideexplained.com/usps-certified-mail-how-it-works/).

## What Registered Mail is

The FAQ lists these characteristics: it is the most secure USPS mail service, protected by safes, cages, sealed containers, locks and keys; it must be presented to a retail employee or rural carrier; and it requires a signature upon delivery. DMM 503.2.1.1 calls it the most secure service the USPS offers and says it incorporates a system of receipts to monitor the movement of the mail from the point of acceptance to delivery.

The DMM says Registered Mail gives the sender a mailing receipt and, on request, electronic verification that an article was delivered or that a delivery attempt was made. The FAQ names PS Form 3806 as the Receipt for Registered Mail. The DMM says the mail must bear a red Label 200, or a non-barcoded Label 200-N when a mailer-generated shipping label with its own barcode (an IMpb or IMmb) is also on the piece; the FAQ names Label 200-N as PS Form 200-N.

Registered Mail Restricted Delivery is a separate option. The FAQ says it lets the mailer direct delivery only to the addressee or the addressee's authorized agent. The DMM adds that the addressee must be an individual (a natural person) specified by name, and that it can be requested at the time of mailing.

## Which mail it goes with

The FAQ says domestic Registered Mail is available only for four mail classes: First-Class Mail, USPS Ground Advantage - Commercial, USPS Ground Advantage - Retail and Priority Mail. It says the expected delivery date is based on the class of mail the item was sent with, and that mail travels at the speed of the class the added service is attached to. It also says Registered Mail is kept highly secured and processed manually, which slows it, and is not recommended if speed of delivery is important.

## Sending it

The DMM says Registered Mail is available only at the time of mailing and must be presented to a retail employee at a Post Office, station or branch (including a contractor-operated unit), or to a rural carrier on a rural route. The FAQ says rural carriers can accept it if the required postage and fees are prepaid. The DMM adds that a postmaster may require an article of unusually high value to be presented only at the main office or at designated stations and branches.

On preparation, the FAQ and the DMM agree: USPS employees are not permitted to help customers prepare or seal mail to be registered, and an envelope or package that appears to have been opened and resealed, or otherwise improperly prepared, may not be registered. The DMM adds that mail must bear the complete names and addresses of both sender and addressee, that envelopes must be securely sealed, and that tape used on Registered Mail must visibly damage the envelope or wrapper if removed. The DMM says registration may not be obtained for mail sent as business reply mail, or presented in a padded envelope, an envelope or mailer made of spun-bonded olefin such as Tyvek, a plastic envelope or mailer, or one made of glossy-coated paper. Check the DMM before preparing the item, because this article quotes only part of its list.

## Declared value

The DMM says the mailer must always declare the item's full value to USPS when presenting it for registration, that the accepting employee may ask for a satisfactory declaration and may refuse to accept the item as Registered Mail without one, and that private insurance does not change the requirement to declare the full value. The FAQ gives the same instruction for insurance: you must declare the full value of the mailpiece when presenting it for mailing, and insurance matching the declared value is included up to a maximum amount that this article does not repeat. Both pages say only an item with no declared value can be registered without postal insurance.

The FAQ and the DMM each give a chart of what to declare. Merchandise is declared at market value or cost, and money at full value. For a nonvaluable item such as a letter or a file, the charts say "no value or replacement cost if postal insurance coverage desired." Both pages also say commercial customers shipping cash above a dollar threshold they state must use Registered Mail. For how a claim works, see [USPS Claims: Filing Periods, Who Can File and What You Need](https://statesideexplained.com/usps-claim-how-it-works/).

## What tracking shows

The FAQ says Registered Mail service and Registered Mail Restricted Delivery service alone cannot be tracked through the mailstream, and that updated scans are not provided as the piece is processed from facility to facility. It says these services do provide delivery information when a scan shows either a delivery status or an attempted delivery, which it says can be retrieved in several ways. For items to APO, FPO or DPO destinations, the FAQ says delivery information is not available. For what the individual status messages mean, see [USPS Tracking Status: What Each One Means and What USPS Says to Do](https://statesideexplained.com/usps-tracking-status-what-it-means/).

The FAQ separately says a system of receipts monitors mail from acceptance to delivery, and that these receipts are not readily available and can only be accessed by starting a claims form, which cannot be completed by phone. The FAQ does not present these receipts as customer tracking.

## Delivery and proof of delivery

The FAQ says Registered Mail requires a signature upon delivery. The DMM says a customer can get a record of delivery, which includes the recipient's signature, by buying a return receipt at the time of mailing. If a return receipt is requested for Registered Mail Restricted Delivery, the DMM says the correct block on Form 3811 must be checked to show that restricted delivery is also required. Return receipts have their own rules and two versions, covered in [USPS Return Receipt: Green Card vs. Electronic Proof of Delivery](https://statesideexplained.com/usps-return-receipt-how-it-works/).

The DMM also says a postmaster may require the addressee to call for Registered Mail at the Post Office if delivery by carrier would not be safe. It says that if an addressee accepts a registered article in bad condition (repaired with sealing stamps or put in a new envelope or wrapper), the addressee must open it without disturbing the seal, in the delivering employee's presence, and that if anything is missing the envelope or wrapper is handed to the employee after it is endorsed to show what was missing.

## If it is lost

The FAQ says the Registered Mail fee is not eligible for a refund after USPS accepts the item, and that if the article is lost and a claim is made, the postage will be refunded but not the Registered Mail fee. The DMM has a separate procedure for Registered Mail with no declared value: only the mailer may file an inquiry, which should be made after 15 days from the mailing date, at usps.com with the article number or by phone, and a duplicate inquiry may not be filed sooner than 30 days after the original. The period for filing the claim itself is a separate rule that this article does not give. For the search route, see [USPS Missing Mail Search: When You Can File and What You Need](https://statesideexplained.com/usps-missing-mail-search-how-it-works/).

## Where the USPS pages differ

- **The word "receipt."** The DMM uses it for the sender's mailing receipt and for the system of receipts that monitors the mail, and the FAQ names PS Form 3806 as the Receipt for Registered Mail while saying the monitoring receipts are not readily available. They are different things.
- **Eligible classes.** The FAQ lists four mail classes in its text. The DMM section says registration may not be obtained for mail that is "not listed as eligible mail under 1.4," an exhibit this article did not read.
- **How soon to ask about a missing item.** Three things are easy to run together. For a search, the Missing Mail FAQ says to allow 14 or more days from the date of mailing for domestic Registered Mail before submitting a search request on the Missing Mail application. The same sentence also names filing a claim, but this article does not read the 14 days as the day a claim can be filed. For an item with no declared value, the DMM has a separate mailer inquiry, to be made after 15 days from the mailing date. An insurance claim follows its own eligibility and filing period, which this article does not give and our claims article covers. The pages read here do not say how these three relate to each other, so this article does not say they are the same.

## What the pages do not say

They do not say how long Registered Mail takes to arrive beyond "based on the class of mail" and "slows" the speed, or what the "several ways" to retrieve delivery information are. They do not say what happens when no one is available to sign. The DMM refers to sections 508.1.1.7 and 508.1.1.8 for delivery of mail marked Restricted Delivery, which this article did not read. The pages give dollar limits and fees that this article leaves out on purpose.

# USPS Registered Mail: Security, Tracking and Delivery Explained

USPS describes Registered Mail as its most secure mail service. It uses a system of receipts to monitor an item from acceptance to delivery, and a signature is required on delivery. That internal system does not mean customers receive facility-by-facility tracking updates: USPS says Registered Mail alone provides delivery or attempted-delivery information, not scans throughout the mailstream.

This article covers domestic Registered Mail using the USPS Registered Mail FAQ and Domestic Mail Manual (DMM) 503.2.0, read November 24, 2026. International rules are outside its scope.

## What does Registered Mail include?

The DMM describes a mailing receipt and, upon request, electronic verification that the article was delivered or a delivery attempt was made. It says USPS uses receipts to monitor movement from acceptance to delivery. The FAQ describes secure handling, including safes, cages, sealed containers, locks and keys.

The FAQ says a signature is required upon delivery. Registered Mail Restricted Delivery is a separate option that directs delivery only to the addressee or the addressee's authorized agent. The DMM says the named addressee for restricted delivery must be an individual.

## Which domestic mail classes are eligible?

The FAQ lists First-Class Mail, Priority Mail, and USPS Ground Advantage, both retail and commercial. Registered Mail is an additional service used with an eligible mail class; the expected delivery date is based on the class chosen. The FAQ cautions that secure handling and manual processing can slow the item, so it is not recommended when speed is the priority.

## How do you send it?

The DMM says Registered Mail is available at the time of mailing and must be presented to a retail employee or a rural carrier under the applicable rules. The FAQ says USPS employees are not permitted to prepare or seal the package for the customer, and improperly prepared or resealed items may not be accepted. The DMM also lists packaging types that cannot be registered. Check the official packaging rules before preparing the item.

USPS identifies PS Form 3806 as the receipt for Registered Mail. The sender must declare the item's full value when presenting it. The FAQ describes insurance matching the declared value up to a limit, subject to its stated conditions; this article does not list amounts or fees.

## What will tracking show?

The FAQ says Registered Mail alone does not provide updated scans as the item is processed from facility to facility. It does provide information when a scan records delivery or an attempted delivery at the destination. For APO, FPO and DPO destinations, the FAQ says delivery information is not available. The system of internal receipts is not a promise that every handling event will appear in customer tracking.

## Can you get proof of delivery?

The DMM says a sender may purchase a Return Receipt at mailing to obtain a delivery record that includes the recipient's signature. Return Receipt has separate conditions and formats. Registered Mail Restricted Delivery is a distinct option for directing delivery to the named addressee or authorized agent.

## What happens if the item is lost?

The FAQ says the Registered Mail fee is not refundable after USPS accepts the item. It also says that if an item is lost and a claim is made, postage is refunded but the Registered Mail fee is not. A claim has its own eligibility and evidence requirements; see [USPS Claims](https://statesideexplained.com/usps-claim-how-it-works/) for the separate claims process.

# USPS Missing Mail Search: When You Can File and What You Need

**USPS says a Missing Mail search request can be filed online starting 7 days after the mailing date and up to 365 days after it.** USPS also says that submitting a search "does not guarantee a successful outcome," and that sometimes it cannot find the item.

This article covers what two USPS pages say about the search: the USPS FAQ "Missing Mail - The Basics" (Article 000007968, dated May 19, 2026) and the USPS.com page "Find Missing Mail" (no update date shown). It also points to a third page, USPS.com "File a Claim," for claims. The wording is as of November 13, 2026. The two search pages overlap but are not identical, and the article shows where they differ instead of choosing one. It gives no prices. It does not cover claims and refunds in detail, the USPS Mail Recovery Center, international mail, or packages that tracking shows as delivered.

## Start with tracking

Both pages put tracking first. USPS.com says that if the package or mail has tracking, check USPS Tracking for its current status before starting a search. The FAQ says to check USPS Tracking before taking the actions it describes. USPS.com adds a line for Informed Delivery users: a mail item may sometimes be delayed, and it points to the Informed Delivery FAQs (our [Informed Delivery article](https://statesideexplained.com/usps-informed-delivery-how-it-works/) covers that service).

The FAQ defines Missing Mail as "mail that has not been delivered by the expected delivery date," which it says includes packages that are undelivered, lost or late. If it has been lost, late or missing for 7 days or more, the FAQ lists four options: submit a search request at [MissingMail.USPS.com](https://missingmail.usps.com/), read the article on the USPS Mail Recovery Center, file a claim (for insured items meeting the appropriate timeframe), or visit a local Post Office.

The FAQ then gives a table by service. In the table below, "wait" is the time the FAQ says to allow, and the last column is what the FAQ says comes next.

| Service in the FAQ table | Wait | What the FAQ says next |
|---|---|---|
| Priority Mail Express | 7 or more days since mailing | If the recipient confirmed it has not arrived, submit a search request and/or email USPS |
| Domestic Registered Mail | Allow 14 or more days from mailing | Submit a search request and/or file a claim |
| Parcel Select | Allow 8 days to arrive; 14 or more days since mailing | If the recipient confirmed it has not arrived, submit a search request and/or email USPS |
| Priority Mail, First-Class Mail with a trackable service, USPS Ground Advantage, and the USPS Tracking, Signature Confirmation and Certified Mail services | None listed in the row | Check the delivery status in USPS Tracking |

The FAQ names Certified Mail as one of the trackable services (see our [Certified Mail article](https://statesideexplained.com/usps-certified-mail-how-it-works/)). For the last row, the only timing the FAQ gives is the 7-day minimum described next. The FAQ's Registered Mail and Parcel Select rows say to allow 14 or more days, while the filing rule below says the application accepts requests from day 7. The FAQ does not say whether a request for those two services is accepted before day 14, so the article does not say either.

## When you can file

According to the FAQ, the earliest the Missing Mail application allows a request is seven days after the original mailing date, and the latest is 365 days after it. The FAQ "strongly" suggests submitting as soon as possible within that 7 to 365 day window. USPS.com gives the start only: requests "can be submitted starting 7 days from mailing."

## What to have ready

The FAQ lists these items: the mailer and recipient addresses; the date of mailing; tracking numbers ("highly recommended, if available"); the type, size and color of the container or packaging (its example is a 12 x 10 brown box); and a detailed description of the contents of each item, with photos if available. USPS.com lists much the same: sender and recipient addresses, the size and type of container, identifying information such as the tracking number or the mailing date from the mailing receipt or Click-N-Ship label receipt, a description of the contents (such as brand, model, color or size), and pictures.

## How to file, and who cannot do it for you

The FAQ says to go to [MissingMail.USPS.com](https://missingmail.usps.com/) (the USPS search request page) and sign in or register, complete the form and submit it. You can save a draft, but drafts expire after 7 days. The FAQ also says that Customer Care representatives are not able to submit a Missing Mail search request for you. USPS.com says USPS sends a confirmation email when it receives the request and periodic updates about the search.

## What USPS says it cannot search for

For the safety of customers and employees, the FAQ says USPS cannot search for or return hazardous material, live ammunition, opened hair or beauty products, over-the-counter drugs, prescription drugs, or perishables and food. The same list includes handguns, with a different note: the FAQ says they "will only be returned to a licensed dealer" on the sender's behalf. USPS.com says a package may not be recovered "because it was not safe to forward," and links to a page on recovery restrictions that this article did not read.

## After you submit

The FAQ lists these statuses: Accepted (the form was received and the inventory search is under way), Mailpiece Found (the item was identified and is being rewrapped and mailed back), Expired (the search form reached its retention period and will no longer be run), Saved as Draft, Rejected (for example by a clerk because it was a duplicate), and Canceled. Updates go by email to the address given under "Whom can we contact about this search?", and there is a final email when the item is returned or the search expires. If an item is found, USPS rewraps it and sends it to the address on the form. If it is not found, the search keeps running until it expires. USPS.com puts it this way: "sometimes we cannot find missing mail items."

A single request can list several items inside one mailpiece, but separate mailpieces need separate requests. Only the contents of a request can be edited; for other errors the FAQ says to cancel the request and submit a new one with the correct information. A request can be canceled from the Missing Mail Search History page, after which no further matches are tried.

## Claims and refunds

USPS.com has two more sections under the search steps. For a service with a money-back guarantee, such as Priority Mail Express, it says you may be able to request a refund. For mail that included insurance, it says you may be able to file an insurance claim. It then adds a sentence that is limited to "damaged or missing contents": for those, USPS recommends filing a claim "immediately," and says "you must file no later than 60 days from the date of mailing." The page ties that 60-day sentence to damaged or missing contents, so it is not treated here as a deadline for every lost or late item. The FAQ's wording is shorter: file a claim "for insured items meeting the appropriate timeframe," with no number.

For the claim rules themselves, USPS has a separate page, [File a Claim](https://www.usps.com/help/claims.htm). It separates two cases: if an item arrives damaged or is missing contents, it says you must file no later than 60 days after the mailing date; if an item does not arrive at all, it says to file in the time period for the service you used, which differs by service. This article gives no claim amounts or filing periods beyond that. The FAQ also lists "Domestic Claims - The Basics" as a related article, which this article did not read.

## What the pages do not say

Neither page gives how long a search stays active: the FAQ says a search expires when it reaches its "retention period" without stating its length. Neither gives how often the periodic updates come, or a rate of how many items are found. Neither mentions a fee for filing a search. The FAQ opens by saying that "whether you are sending it or receiving it" the mail is important, but neither page says in so many words who may file. USPS.com shows no update date. USPS Package Intercept, a separate service that tries to stop an eligible package before delivery, is covered in our [Package Intercept article](https://statesideexplained.com/usps-package-intercept-how-it-works/).

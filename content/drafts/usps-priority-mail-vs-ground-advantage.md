# Priority Mail vs. Ground Advantage: What USPS Pages Say

**USPS's Priority Mail page gives an expected delivery of 2-3 days and its USPS Ground Advantage page an expected 2-5 days, and the two pages differ on size limits, included insurance and other conditions.** The Priority Mail page says its expected delivery date "does not come with a money-back guarantee"; the Ground Advantage page does not say either way.

This article compares three USPS.com pages as of November 17, 2026: "Priority Mail" (usps.com/ship/priority-mail.htm), "Ground Advantage" (usps.com/ship/ground-advantage.htm) and "Shipping Insurance and Delivery Services" (usps.com/ship/insurance-extra-services.htm). None of the three shows an update date, and this article did not find a USPS FAQ article for Ground Advantage. It gives no prices; all three pages show them. It does not cover Priority Mail Express, business and commercial options, international mail, or which service to pick. Where a fact appears on only one page, the article says so and does not assume the other service is the same.

## Delivery time

The Priority Mail page is headed "2-3 Days" and says the service "includes tracking and delivery in 2-3 days." Its footnote says that "in most cases" the expected delivery date printed on your receipt or provided at checkout "will reflect a delivery time of 2-3 days and is based on origin, destination, and drop-off time," and adds: "The expected delivery date does not come with a money-back guarantee." The page also points to the USPS Service Standards Map for delivery time estimates.

The Ground Advantage page is headed "Packages in 2–5 Days," and its footnote reads "Expected delivery in 2–5 days." The footnote then says that "packages going to certain places (including Alaska, Hawaii, and offshore destinations), or containing hazardous materials may receive slower service." Both pages word the time as an expected delivery, so this article reads 2-3 and 2-5 days as USPS's expected times, not as fixed delivery times. The Ground Advantage page does not say how much slower, and it does not say whether its expected delivery date comes with a money-back guarantee or not. It also does not say where the date is shown, or that it depends on drop-off time, as the Priority Mail footnote does. This article therefore does not say Ground Advantage has no guarantee; it says the page is silent on it.

For December, see our article on the [USPS 2026 holiday shipping deadlines](https://statesideexplained.com/usps-holiday-shipping-deadlines-2026/), which covers USPS's recommended send-by dates for both services.

## Size and weight

Both pages give 70 lbs as the maximum weight. The largest combined length and girth (girth is the distance around the thickest part) is 108 inches on the Priority Mail page and 130 inches on the Ground Advantage page.

Packaging differs. The Priority Mail page describes Flat Rate envelopes and boxes in standard sizes that "let you ship packages up to 70 lbs to any state at the same price," or your own box priced by weight and zone. The Ground Advantage page says you need to use your own packaging and measure it, and prices by weight and zone: up to 15.999 oz in tiers of 4 oz, 8 oz, 12 oz and 15.999 oz, then by the pound, rounded up. It also says a large First-Class Mail envelope that is more than 15" long, 12" high or 3/4" thick, rigid, or not uniformly thick pays the Ground Advantage price.

Both pages describe dimensional weight the same way: for a package larger than 1 cubic foot (1,728 cubic inches), multiply length by width by height and divide by 139. Both also list extra fees for nonstandard packages and for live animals or perishable items such as food or plants. The two lists of nonstandard fees are not the same, and this article does not give them. The Priority Mail page adds that Flat Rate packaging is exempt from all nonstandard fees.

## Insurance and tracking

The two pages word the insurance differently. The Priority Mail page lists "USPS Tracking and up to $100 of insurance with most shipments (restrictions apply)." The Ground Advantage page says "$100 insurance is included (for both outbound and return shipments)," and that you can buy up to $5,000 in additional coverage against loss or damage for merchandise.

The conditions are on the Priority Mail page's footnotes and on the insurance page. The Priority Mail footnote says insurance is "available only for certain products and to certain destinations," that "insurance does not cover certain items," and points to the Domestic Mail Manual: 609.4.3 (Non-payable Claims), 609 (Filing Indemnity Claims for Loss or Damage) and 503.4 (Insured Mail). It says Priority Mail domestic shipments must have the applicable USPS Tracking barcode to qualify; that insurance is not included for Priority Mail Open & Distribute, Premium Forwarding Service-Residential or Premium Forwarding Service-Commercial; and that when additional insurance is bought, "it replaces the included insurance."

The insurance page says that both Priority Mail and Ground Advantage domestic shipments "must have an applicable USPS Tracking barcode to qualify for included insurance," and that Priority Mail insurance "is not included when you purchase it along with certain extra services." Its footnote says the included coverage is limited to a maximum liability of $100 when the piece bears an Intelligent Mail package barcode or USPS retail tracking barcode "and the mailer pays retail or commercial prices." The page does not name the extra services it means. Separately, section 503 of the Domestic Mail Manual says: "Except for Priority Mail pieces with included insurance, no insurance coverage is provided when purchasing Certified Mail." Our [Certified Mail article](https://statesideexplained.com/usps-certified-mail-how-it-works/) covers that service.

Tracking is included in both. The Priority Mail page excludes Prepaid Forever Priority Mail Flat Rate products from tracking; none of the three pages says whether those packages get the included insurance, so the article does not say so either. For what tracking statuses mean, see our [tracking status article](https://statesideexplained.com/usps-tracking-status-what-it-means/). If a package does not arrive, USPS.com asks you to keep your postmarked mailing receipt and proof of value in case you need a claim; our [Missing Mail article](https://statesideexplained.com/usps-missing-mail-search-how-it-works/) covers the search for lost mail.

## Pickup and other features

Both pages list free Package Pickup at your home or office. Our [Package Pickup article](https://statesideexplained.com/usps-package-pickup-how-it-works/) covers how it works and its limits, including that the FAQ lists both Priority Mail and USPS Ground Advantage as eligible.

Some lines appear on one page only. The Priority Mail page says there are "no surcharges for fuel; residential or rural delivery; or Saturday delivery." The footnote attached to the Saturday part mentions Priority Mail Express, so this article draws no conclusion from the Saturday wording. The Ground Advantage page says it "includes forwarding, undeliverable as addressed (UAA), and return to sender (RTS) at no additional cost"; the Priority Mail page has no such line. The Ground Advantage page calls it the "primary option" for hazardous materials that are only mailable by ground transportation, and says its HAZMAT handling fee is zero. The Priority Mail page lists a HAZMAT handling fee.

The Ground Advantage page calls the service "our best-priced service for items up to 15.999 oz." This article does not compare costs because it gives no prices; USPS.com has a Price Calculator for that.

## What the pages do not say

None of the three pages shows an update date. The Ground Advantage page does not say whether its expected date has a guarantee, how much slower "slower service" can be, or whether the date depends on drop-off time. The Priority Mail page lists some exclusions in its footnotes but says restrictions and exclusions apply and points to the Domestic Mail Manual for the rest; this article read only the claims section of the manual (609) and the one sentence of section 503 quoted above, not 503.4 itself. Whether Prepaid Forever Priority Mail Flat Rate packages get the included insurance is not stated. Neither page says which service is better for a given package.

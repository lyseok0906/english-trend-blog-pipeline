# Can You Bring Alcohol on a Plane? What TSA and the FAA Say

**TSA says alcoholic beverages are allowed in checked bags and in carry-on bags, but carry-on amounts are limited to 3.4 oz (100 ml).** In checked bags, TSA says beverages with more than 24% but not more than 70% alcohol are limited to 5 liters (1.3 gallons) per passenger and must be in unopened retail packaging. TSA says beverages with 24% alcohol or less are not subject to limitations in checked bags.

This article covers what TSA's and the FAA's pages say. The wording is as of October 13, 2026, and comes from the three pages listed at the end. It describes the rules and does not give advice on what to pack.

## What TSA says for carry-on and checked bags

| Where | What TSA says |
|---|---|
| Carry-on bag | Allowed in amounts of 3.4 oz (100 ml) or less |
| Checked bag, 24% alcohol or less | Not subject to limitations |
| Checked bag, more than 24% but not more than 70% alcohol | Limited to 5 liters (1.3 gallons) per passenger, in unopened retail packaging |

For carry-on bags, TSA's page adds that mini bottles of alcohol must be able to comfortably fit into a single quart-sized bag. For the general carry-on liquids rule, see [the TSA 3-1-1 liquids rule](https://statesideexplained.com/tsa-3-1-1-liquids-rule/).

## What the FAA and the regulation add

The FAA's PackSafe page gives the same figures for beverages with more than 24% but not more than 70% alcohol by volume (up to 140 proof). It says they must be in unopened retail packaging, with a limit of 5 liters per packaging and 5 liters in total per passenger. A footnote says liquids, gels and aerosols in carry-on baggage are further limited to 100-ml (3.4 oz) containers at the TSA security checkpoint.

Both TSA and the FAA point to the regulation, 49 CFR 175.10(a)(4). It lists alcoholic beverages among the hazardous materials that the subchapter's rules do not apply to when aircraft passengers carry them and the section's requirements are met. It covers beverages with not more than 24% alcohol by volume, and beverages with more than 24% and not more than 70% alcohol by volume in unopened retail packagings not exceeding 5 liters, carried in carry-on or checked baggage, with a total of 5 liters per person. The FAA page and the regulation use alcohol by volume. TSA's page says only "alcohol."

## Beverages with more than 70% alcohol

TSA's page does not address beverages above 70% alcohol. The FAA's page has a separate heading, "Alcoholic Beverages Over 140 Proof," that defines them as greater than 70% alcohol by volume, including grain alcohol and 151 proof rum, and refers to the regulation. The page text under that heading states no rule. The regulation's exception, as written, covers beverages up to 70% alcohol by volume. This article does not state a rule for stronger beverages.

## Drinking alcohol on board

TSA says FAA regulations prohibit travelers from consuming alcohol on board an aircraft unless it is served by a flight attendant. The FAA's page says passengers may not drink alcohol on board unless it is served by the air carrier. TSA adds that flight attendants are not permitted to serve a passenger who is intoxicated.

## Your airline and the TSA officer

TSA says to check with your airline before bringing any alcoholic beverages on board. TSA's page also says the final decision rests with the TSA officer on whether an item is allowed through the checkpoint.

## What this article does not cover

This article does not cover duty-free purchases, flights arriving from other countries, any airline's own policy, or state and foreign laws about alcohol. It does not cover alcohol carried for sale, how to pack bottles, or how much to bring. It states no rule for beverages over 70% alcohol. This article describes what the three pages state on the date above and does not track changes to them.

# How Does USPS Package Pickup Work? Free Pickup and Limits

**Package Pickup is a free USPS service: your letter carrier collects eligible prepaid packages from your address on the next delivery day or a day you choose.** USPS's FAQ says it costs nothing regardless of how many items you schedule, and that you can schedule, edit or cancel it Monday through Saturday until 2:00 AM Central Time on the day of the pickup. Each item can weigh up to 70 pounds and measure up to 130 inches in total length and girth. Pickup on Demand, which lets you choose a two-hour window, is a separate service with a fee.

This article covers what three USPS pages say: the FAQ "Package Pickup & Pickup on Demand," the USPS.com page "How to Prepare & Send a Package" and USPS's "Schedule a Pickup" page. The wording is as of November 9, 2026. None of the pages shows an update date. The article gives no prices or fee amounts and instead names the USPS price list the FAQ points to, and it does not cover international mail in detail, business mailers, USPS Connect or how a particular carrier handles a stop.

## Package Pickup and Pickup on Demand side by side

USPS's FAQ describes three options for outgoing mail: Pickup on Demand, Package Pickup and Collection Service, which means prepaid mail dropped in a collection box or handed to a letter carrier or other postal employee. The two scheduled services differ as follows.

| | Package Pickup | Pickup on Demand |
|---|---|---|
| Cost | Free, regardless of the number of items | A fee per trip, regardless of the number of items; the FAQ says fees are in Notice 123 |
| When | Next delivery day or a designated delivery day, at the time the carrier delivers your mail; can occur at any time on the scheduled date | A specific date within a two-hour window; typically occurs within two hours of the scheduled time |
| Schedule, edit or cancel by | Monday to Saturday until 2:00 AM Central Time on the day of the pickup | Monday to Saturday until 5:00 AM local time on the day of the pickup |
| Mail that is eligible | The list in the next section | The same list plus USPS Connect Local, Package Service parcels, and Priority Mail Express and Priority Mail Open and Distribute shipments |

## What can be picked up

The FAQ says the letter carrier can only pick up packages with prepaid postage, or return merchandise, that are eligible for the free or the paid service. For Package Pickup it lists Priority Mail Express, Priority Mail, USPS Ground Advantage, returns, International Priority Mail Express, International Priority Mail and First-Class Package International Service. It adds that packages of other mail classes, such as First-Class Mail items, may also be picked up if they are combined with one of those products.

Media Mail is not named on that list, and this article does not say whether a Media Mail package can be picked up. The rules for that service are in [What Can You Send by USPS Media Mail? What Qualifies](https://statesideexplained.com/usps-media-mail-what-qualifies/).

Two notes in the FAQ apply to specific services. For Priority Mail Express, the guarantee for a specific mailing requires that the item be presented to an acceptance employee at a Post Office, and the FAQ says to contact the local Post Office for guarantee details. International packages can be picked up only if the shipping label, customs forms and postage payment were completed online; packages with hand-written customs forms must be taken to a Post Office retail location.

## Postage and the 10-ounce rule

The FAQ says sufficient postage must be on the package before the pickup. You can buy postage online through Click-N-Ship, PC Postage or postage meters. The valid ways to prepay that it lists are postage stamps (if the item weighs 10 oz or less and is 1/2 inch or less in thickness), a postage meter imprint, PC Postage products, online Click-N-Ship labels with postage, a prepaid Priority Mail Flat Rate envelope and return items that are prepaid.

A mailpiece that weighs more than 10 oz or is more than 1/2 inch thick and has stamps for postage must be presented by the customer to an employee at a Post Office retail counter. The USPS.com page adds that a stamped package over those limits put in a mailbox is left by the carrier and one dropped in a blue collection box is returned to the sender, and that if you print the postage with Click-N-Ship or at a USPS kiosk you can use any drop-off method. The FAQ also says the letter carrier is not required to verify postage, but that offices follow standard revenue protection practices if a package appears underpaid.

## Size, hazards and addresses

Each item can weigh up to 70 pounds and measure up to 130 inches in total length and girth. The FAQ says hazardous materials must meet USPS packaging, labeling and other mailability standards in the Domestic Mail Manual or International Mail Manual, and that this includes items that contain anything fragile without proper packaging or liquid items.

The FAQ says not all addresses are eligible for Package Pickup, and USPS's Schedule a Pickup page says it will verify that the address is eligible. The pages do not list which addresses are not eligible.

## Scheduling, changing and leaving the package

You can submit a request on a weekend or holiday, but the pickup happens on the next scheduled delivery day; USPS's example is a Saturday-afternoon request accepted for Monday unless Monday is a Postal holiday. You can schedule up to 6 months in advance, and a login is needed for future and repeat requests, including daily, weekly or monthly pickups within the next six months.

When you request a pickup you choose where the package will be left. The FAQ lists locations such as in or at the mailbox, on the porch, the front, back or side door, knock on the door or ring the bell, the mail room, an office or reception. For multi-story buildings it asks residents to bring packages to a ground-level, secured location. It says letter carriers may use their discretion and that snow, ice, obstructed pathways and dogs may prevent a pickup. You do not need to be present, as long as you are shipping postage-paid items within the United States that have been properly prepared for pickup and left in a safe location for the carrier.

To change or cancel, the FAQ says to open the Schedule a Pickup application, choose "Modify an Existing Pickup" and enter the confirmation number with the email address or phone number. You can modify the date, the location, the number of packages and the total weight. If the package volume or weight rises on the same day as the pickup, the FAQ says to call the local Post Office. You get a confirmation email or, if no email address was given, a notice from the carrier after the pickup; if the item was not collected, the notice says why. Delivery status online is available for Priority Mail Express items and packages with USPS Tracking or Signature Confirmation.

## If the pickup does not happen

The FAQ says to contact the local Post Office and to file a service request through "Email Us," then "Where is my Mail?" It adds that Pickup on Demand refunds can be requested online if the service was not performed according to its service features.

## What the pages do not say

None of the three pages shows an update date. The FAQ gives the cutoff for Package Pickup as 2:00 AM Central Time in one place, while its note on modifying or canceling says "before 2:00 am" without a time zone; this article uses the Central Time wording and does not resolve the difference. The pages do not list the addresses that are not eligible, do not say what time of day a carrier will arrive, and do not give the Pickup on Demand fee, which the FAQ places in Notice 123.

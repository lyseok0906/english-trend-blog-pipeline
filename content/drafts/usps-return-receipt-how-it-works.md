# USPS Return Receipt: Green Card vs. Electronic Proof of Delivery

**USPS pages describe a Return Receipt as proof of delivery for the sender: the recipient's signature, the date of delivery and information about the actual delivery address if it differs, which must be bought at the time of mailing and comes either as a mailed green card (PS Form 3811) or as an email.** USPS says a mailer can no longer buy one after mailing, but a sender who paid for one and did not get it can ask for information from the delivery record within 90 days of purchase. This article compares what the pages say, including where they word things differently.

This article uses the USPS FAQ "Return Receipt - The Basics" (Article 000006076, dated September 18, 2026), the USPS FAQ "Electronic Return Receipt" (Article 000007257, dated May 18, 2026) and section 6.0 of Domestic Mail Manual (DMM) 503, "Additional Mailing Services" (no update date shown), all read on November 24, 2026. It gives no fees or prices, and it does not repeat how Certified Mail works, which is in our article [What Is USPS Certified Mail? What It Includes and How It Works](https://statesideexplained.com/usps-certified-mail-how-it-works/).

## What a Return Receipt shows

The FAQ says a Return Receipt provides the sender with proof of delivery: the recipient's signature, along with information about the delivery address if it is different, and the date and time of delivery. It lists the proof as the date of delivery, the signature of the recipient or the recipient's authorized agent, and information about the actual delivery address if it differs. The DMM words it as evidence of delivery, "to whom the mail was delivered and date of delivery," with information about the recipient's actual delivery address.

## The two versions side by side

| | Green card (PS Form 3811) | Electronic Return Receipt |
|---|---|---|
| What the sender gets | A mailed card, the hard copy, sent to the sender after delivery | An email with a link to USPS Tracking and a proof of delivery letter attached |
| When it is requested | At the time of mailing | At the time of mailing; the email is requested afterward on the USPS Tracking website |
| Where to buy it | Not stated; the Basics FAQ says a mailpiece with the correct fees, postage and form affixed may be mailed from home or office or dropped in a collection box | At a Post Office; the FAQ says it currently cannot be bought through USPS.com |
| Viewed online | The FAQ says a Return Receipt cannot be viewed online | Delivered by email |
| Not available for | Not stated | Items to APO, FPO or DPO addresses, or to U.S. territories, possessions and freely associated states |

## Buying it at the time of mailing

The Basics FAQ says a Return Receipt may be purchased at the time of mailing, and that a mailer can no longer purchase one after mailing. The DMM adds the following for the green card.

- A complete return address is required on the mailpiece. For Priority Mail Express, the return address on the PME label meets this requirement.
- The mailer completes the entries on PS Form 3811, including the article identification number, and attaches the form to the article.
- The name and address for the return of the receipt must be the mailer's or the mailer's agent, except for certain restricted mailings of electronic nicotine delivery systems, cigarettes and smokeless tobacco.
- Mail with a Form 3811 must be endorsed "Return Receipt Requested" above the delivery address and to the right of the return address. No endorsement is required for electronic return receipt service.
- The weight of the return receipt is not counted when postage is calculated.

## Which mail it goes with

The Basics FAQ says Return Receipt (PS Form 3811) is available for Priority Mail Express (marked "Form 3811 only"), Priority Mail, First-Class Mail, USPS Ground Advantage - Commercial and Parcel Select. After Parcel Select it puts a condition in parentheses, "if purchased with" Certified Mail, Collect on Delivery (COD), insurance over a value the FAQ states, or Registered Mail, and it does not say whether the condition covers the classes listed before it. It lists further classes, including Bound Printed Matter, Library Mail and Media Mail, when bought with COD or insurance over that value. For international mail, the FAQ says a Return Receipt is available for First-Class Mail International only with Registered Mail to certain destinations, using PS Form 2865, a pink card.

The same FAQ lists extra services that can be combined with a Return Receipt: Certified Mail (including its restricted delivery and adult signature variants), Restricted Delivery, COD, Registered Mail, USPS Tracking, Signature Confirmation, insurance above the stated value and Adult Signature services. Some of these are marked "Form 3811 only." The Electronic Return Receipt FAQ lists the domestic classes in the same way and also marks Priority Mail Express "Form 3811 only." The DMM's sentence about the PME return address carries the note "Form 3811 option only." Our [USPS Redelivery: How to Schedule It, Cutoffs and Return Dates](https://statesideexplained.com/usps-redelivery-how-it-works/) article lists Return Receipt among the items that get a redelivery notice.

## Getting the electronic version

The Electronic Return Receipt FAQ says it must be requested at the time of mailing and that USPS must wait for delivery and for the signature record to be complete before it can email it. To get the delivery record, the FAQ says to go to the USPS Tracking website, enter the Certified Mail, COD, insured (over the stated value) or Registered Mail article number from the label or receipt, and enter an email address when the Return Receipt (Electronic) option appears. The Basics FAQ gives the same steps for requesting it again: enter the tracking number in USPS Tracking, select "Return Receipt Electronic" under "Available Actions," and provide an email address. Without the article barcode number, the FAQ says the electronic version cannot be obtained.

On timing, the FAQ says that if the record is requested for an article that has already been delivered and the paperwork is complete, it is processed immediately and the email should arrive within hours depending on the email server. If it is requested before delivery, USPS waits, and the letter is usually available to be sent within 48 hours of delivery. The email includes a link to USPS Tracking with the delivery information filled in, and the signature arrives as a proof of delivery letter attached to the email.

The FAQ calls the electronic version an official USPS document "designed to be equivalent to" the hard copy, and says its legal status "is not determined by the Postal Service," because that decision is made by individual courts. Records are kept for two years from the date of mailing, and after 60 days the record will take longer to retrieve. For senders who mail large volumes, such as 25 items a day, the FAQ describes the USPS Bulk Proof of Delivery Program, which provides signature data in an extract file.

## Tracking the green card itself

The Basics FAQ says PS Form 3811 has a barcode with a "Related Tracking Number." That number is not for tracking the mailpiece. Entering it into USPS Tracking provides information about the hard copy return receipt while it is being mailed to the sender. The FAQ also says mailers can use USPS Tracking to check the status of the delivery record after delivery of a mailpiece with a hard copy Return Receipt.

## If the Return Receipt did not arrive

For a green card that was paid for and did not arrive, the FAQ and the DMM say the mailer can request information from the delivery record within 90 days of the date of purchase. The mailer completes PS Form 3811-A at any Post Office, station or branch and shows the receipt proving the Return Receipt fee was paid.

For a refund, the Basics FAQ says Return Receipt fees are refunded only if USPS fails to provide the recipient's signature, unless the item was otherwise refused, unclaimed or returned to the sender. It says a refund request goes through usps.com/help or the origin Post Office "not less than 30 days, or more than 60 days from the date of mailing." It also says that for a Return Receipt bought at a retail Post Office, a sender who has not received it or got an incomplete one may request a refund or a replacement record after 30 calendar days from the date of mailing, or any time after they know the item was delivered. For an electronic receipt that has not arrived within 3 to 5 days of the request, the FAQ suggests checking the delivery status on USPS.com, looking in the junk folder, and requesting it again through USPS Tracking. For the general refund rules and windows, see [USPS Refunds: What Can Be Refunded, When to Ask and What You Need](https://statesideexplained.com/usps-refund-how-it-works/).

## Where the USPS pages differ

- **When the email should arrive.** The Electronic Return Receipt FAQ says the letter is usually available within 48 hours of delivery. The Basics FAQ says that if an electronic receipt has not arrived within 3 to 5 days of the request, check the delivery status. The pages do not say how the two fit together.
- **How the email address is given.** The Basics FAQ says a mailer may choose to receive the receipt "by email." The DMM says a mailer may choose electronic delivery by email or by signature extract file. The Electronic Return Receipt FAQ says the email address is entered later on the USPS Tracking website, not at purchase, and that the electronic version cannot be bought through USPS.com.
- **Refund timing.** One sentence says a refund request is made "not less than 30 days, or more than 60 days from the date of mailing." The next says a sender who bought at a Post Office may ask for a refund or replacement after 30 calendar days from mailing, or any time after the item is known to be delivered. The pages do not say which applies to a given sender.
- **Priority Mail Express.** The FAQ and the DMM say Form 3811 only for PME. The Electronic Return Receipt FAQ lists PME with the same "Form 3811 only" note. The pages do not spell out whether PME can have the electronic version.

## What the pages do not say

They do not say how long a green card takes to reach the sender after delivery. They do not say whether a court will accept a Return Receipt in a particular case. The FAQ only says courts decide the legal status of the electronic version. For fees the Basics FAQ points to Notice 123 and the "Insurance & Extra Services" page, which this article does not read or give.

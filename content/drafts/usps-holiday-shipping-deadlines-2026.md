# USPS Holiday Shipping Deadlines 2026: Send-By Dates for Christmas

**For delivery before Dec. 25, USPS recommends sending Lower 48 mail by Thursday, Dec. 17 with USPS Ground Advantage or First-Class Mail, by Friday, Dec. 18 with Priority Mail, or by Saturday, Dec. 19 with Priority Mail Express.** International and military dates are earlier, and USPS says the actual delivery date may vary.

This article reads three USPS sources: the USPS.com page "2026 Holiday Shipping Deadlines," a USPS news release dated September 22, 2026, and the USPS FAQ "2026 Holiday Mailing Deadlines," dated October 14, 2026. The wording is as of November 13, 2026. The USPS.com page shows no update date. The three sources give the same send-by dates; they differ in some footnotes, shown below. The article gives no prices and does not cover Post Office hours, delivery on or around Christmas, other carriers or business mailers.

## Lower 48 states, Alaska, Hawaii, Puerto Rico and territories

USPS describes these as "recommended send-by dates for expected delivery before December 25." The first column is the contiguous U.S. (the Lower 48 states); the second is Alaska, Hawaii, Puerto Rico and U.S. territories.

| Service | Lower 48 | AK, HI, Puerto Rico, territories |
|---|---|---|
| USPS Ground Advantage | Thu, Dec. 17 | No date; see below |
| First-Class Mail | Thu, Dec. 17 | Thu, Dec. 17 |
| Priority Mail | Fri, Dec. 18 | Fri, Dec. 18 |
| Priority Mail Express | Sat, Dec. 19 | Sat, Dec. 19 |

The FAQ labels the First-Class Mail row "including greeting cards." For USPS Ground Advantage to or from Alaska, Hawaii, Puerto Rico and the territories, the table gives no date. A footnote says packages between the contiguous United States and Alaska, Hawaii, American Samoa, Puerto Rico, the U.S. Virgin Islands, Guam, the Northern Mariana Islands and the Freely Associated States "will have a 10-day or more service expectation," that days vary with distance, and that customers can look up service expectations from one ZIP Code to another for a particular mailing date at postcalc.usps.com/ServiceCommitments. Another footnote says Ground Advantage packages to or from these ZIP Codes may have longer transit times and extended estimated delivery dates, and to adjust the induction date as needed.

A third footnote says U.S. Customs and Border Protection requires mailers to prepay duties, taxes and fees, and to obtain proof of payment, on articles valued up to $2,500 entering the customs territory of the United States from American Samoa, Guam, the Northern Mariana Islands and the U.S. Virgin Islands, and points to the Domestic Mail Manual for details.

## International mail

For international mail, USPS lists the same dates for each of nine destination groups: Africa, Asia/Pacific Rim, Australia/New Zealand, Canada, the Caribbean, Central and South America, Europe, Mexico and the Middle East.

| Service | Send by |
|---|---|
| First-Class Package International Service | Wed, Dec. 9 |
| Priority Mail International | Wed, Dec. 9 |
| Priority Mail Express International | Wed, Dec. 16 |

The FAQ adds that the actual delivery date may vary "including customs delays." The USPS.com page's note on this table does not mention customs.

## Military mail (APO, FPO and DPO)

| Service | ZIPs 090-092, 094-099, 340, 962-966 | ZIP 093 |
|---|---|---|
| USPS Ground Advantage | Wed, Nov. 4 | Wed, Nov. 4 |
| First-Class Mail | Wed, Dec. 9 | Wed, Dec. 9 |
| Priority Mail | Wed, Dec. 9 | Wed, Dec. 9 |
| Priority Mail Express Military Service | Wed, Dec. 16 | N/A |

USPS's note says Priority Mail Express Military Service is available to selected military and diplomatic Post Offices and to check with the local Post Office whether it is available for an APO, FPO or DPO address. The Ground Advantage date of November 4 has already passed as of the date of this article. The September 22 release says USPS recommends visiting the holiday shipping page for the full list of military and international dates beginning mid-October.

## What "recommended" means

The pages call these "recommended" dates. Footnote 1 of the USPS.com page and the FAQ say the actual delivery date may vary depending on origin, destination, Post Office acceptance time (the FAQ says acceptance date and time) and other conditions. The September 22 release says customers "should always plan to mail and ship packages early to ensure arrival by Dec. 25."

The pages differ on the guarantee for Priority Mail Express. The USPS.com page says that as of August 1, 2023, USPS no longer processes refund requests for guaranteed service on these items, and that refund requests for loss are still processed. The FAQ says that, excluding Puerto Rico, USPS does not process refund requests for guaranteed service on these items. The FAQ also says that for Priority Mail Express 1-Day shipments mailed December 22 through December 25, postage for the money-back guaranteed service is not refunded if the shipment was delivered, or delivery was attempted, within two business days of the date of mailing. This article does not reconcile the wording.

The USPS.com page also says that Hanukkah (Dec. 4 to Dec. 12) is earlier this year, so please adjust accordingly; it gives no send-by dates specific to Hanukkah or Kwanzaa. It also points to free shipping supplies, printing labels and scheduling a free package pickup; how pickup works is covered in [How Does USPS Package Pickup Work? Free Pickup and Limits](https://statesideexplained.com/usps-package-pickup-how-it-works/).

## What the pages do not say

The USPS.com page shows no update date; the release is dated September 22, 2026 and the FAQ October 14, 2026. None of the three pages gives a time of day by which a Post Office must accept an item, and none gives Post Office hours. They give no send-by dates for other mail types or for returns, and no prices. For USPS Ground Advantage to Alaska, Hawaii, Puerto Rico and the territories, they give a service expectation of 10 days or more and a lookup tool, not a send-by date.

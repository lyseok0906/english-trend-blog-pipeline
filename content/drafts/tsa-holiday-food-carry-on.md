# Can You Bring Holiday Food Through TSA? What TSA Says About Thanksgiving Dishes

**TSA's Food page lists pies, cakes, cookies and cooked meat as allowed in both carry-on and checked bags, and allows gravy, jam, maple syrup and sauces in a carry-on at 3.4 ounces (100 ml) or less.** TSA's liquids page says that limit applies to each container, not to a total. A TSA local press release from Harrisburg International Airport, dated November 24, 2025, gives a holiday list and says to pack gravy, cranberry sauce, jams and maple syrup in checked bags. The two TSA sources word some items differently, and this article keeps them apart.

This article covers what TSA says about holiday food in two places: the Food list on TSA's "What Can I Bring?" pages, and the 2025 local release from Harrisburg International Airport. It also uses a third TSA page, "Liquids, Aerosols, and Gels Rule," only to explain what the 3.4-ounce limit refers to. All three were read on November 4, 2026. The Food page and the Liquids page show no date. On that day TSA's press-release list showed no 2026 Thanksgiving release (the newest item was dated October 28, 2026), so the holiday examples below are from 2025 and TSA may say something different this year. The article does not cover airline rules, food import rules, alcohol or food safety.

## What TSA's Food page says

The Food page lists 59 items. Each has a carry-on value and a checked-bag value. None of the items is named cranberry sauce, stuffing, casserole or turkey. These are the rows that matter most for holiday food, with the values as the page shows them:

| Item on the Food page | Carry-on | Checked |
|---|---|---|
| Pies and Cakes | Yes | Yes |
| Cookies | Yes | Yes |
| Cooked Meat, Seafood and Vegetable (No Liquid) | Yes | Yes |
| Bread; Candy; Spices (dry) | Yes | Yes |
| Fresh Fruits and Vegetables | Yes (Special Instructions) | Yes |
| Fresh Meat and Seafood; Frozen Food | Yes (Special Instructions) | Yes |
| Gravy | Yes (Less than or equal to 3.4oz/100 ml allowed) | Yes |
| Jam and Jelly; Maple Syrup | Yes (Less than or equal to 3.4oz/100 ml allowed) | Yes |
| Salsa and Sauces; Soups | Yes (Less than or equal to 3.4oz/100 ml allowed) | Yes |
| Canned Foods | Yes (Special Instructions) | Yes |

Several rows say that solid food items (not liquids or gels) can be transported in either carry-on or checked bags. The Fresh Fruits and Vegetables row says this applies within the continental United States, and adds that passengers flying from Hawaii, Puerto Rico or the U.S. Virgin Islands to the mainland cannot take most fresh fruits and vegetables. This article does not go further into that point.

For fresh meat and frozen food, the page says that if the food is packed with ice or ice packs, they must be completely frozen when brought through screening, and that partially melted ice or ice packs with liquid at the bottom will not be permitted. For canned foods, the page says they could require additional screening that might result in the item not being allowed through the checkpoint, and TSA suggests packing them in a checked bag, shipping them or leaving them at home.

The Food page writes the limit as "Yes (Less than or equal to 3.4oz/100 ml allowed)" and does not say what the 3.4 ounces is measured against. TSA's "Liquids, Aerosols, and Gels Rule" page does. It says liquids, aerosols, gels, creams and pastes in a carry-on are limited to travel-sized containers of 3.4 ounces (100 milliliters) or less per item, kept in a quart-sized bag, and that items in larger containers go in checked baggage. So the 3.4 ounces is the size of each container, not a total for the trip. The pages read do not go further, for example into a larger container that is only partly full, and this article does not either.

## What TSA's November 2025 local release says

On November 24, 2025, TSA at Harrisburg International Airport (MDT) published a local press release titled "TSA prepared for busy Thanksgiving travel in Harrisburg, offers special packing tips." It says most foods can be carried through a TSA checkpoint, but some items must be transported in checked baggage. It also says that passengers carrying items that can spill, spread, spray, pump or pour, and that are larger than 3.4 ounces, should pack those in checked luggage.

The release gives these examples of holiday food that travelers can carry through a checkpoint: baked goods (homemade or store-bought pies, cakes, cookies, brownies and other sweet treats); meats, including turkey, chicken, ham and steak, frozen, cooked or uncooked; stuffing, cooked or uncooked, in a box or bag; solid casseroles, which can go in carry-on or checked luggage; mac and cheese that is solid and already cooked in a pan, or the ingredients to cook at the destination; fresh vegetables; fresh fruit, with cranberries among the examples; candy; and spices. It adds that casseroles with liquid or semi-liquid components, such as soups, sauces or gravy, must comply with TSA's 3-1-1 liquids rule, and that each liquid or semi-liquid item must be in a container of 3.4 ounces or less and fit into a single quart-sized, clear, resealable plastic bag.

It gives these examples of holiday food to pack in checked baggage: cranberry sauce, homemade or canned, "as it is spreadable"; gravy, homemade or in a jar or can; canned fruits or vegetables in liquid-filled cans or jars; preserves, jams and jellies, where it says spreadable items must be checked; and maple syrup. For cold items, it says ice packs are permitted but must be frozen solid when going through screening.

Other TSA airports published local releases with similar titles on November 24 and 25, 2025. Only the Harrisburg text was read for this article, and the others were not compared with it. TSA's national release of November 21, 2025 gave general holiday travel tips and contained no food examples.

## Where the two TSA sources differ

The Food page and the Harrisburg release do not word every item the same way:

| Item | TSA Food page | Harrisburg release, Nov. 24, 2025 |
|---|---|---|
| Gravy | Carry-on: Yes, 3.4 oz or less (container size, see above). Checked: Yes | Pack in checked baggage (homemade or in a jar or can) |
| Jam and jelly | Carry-on: Yes, 3.4 oz or less (container size, see above). Checked: Yes | Preserves, jams and jellies: spreadable items must be checked |
| Maple syrup | Carry-on: Yes, 3.4 oz or less (container size, see above). Checked: Yes | Listed as a checked-baggage example |
| Cranberry sauce | No row on the page | Checked baggage, "as it is spreadable" |

Both sources point to checked bags for larger amounts. The Food page also shows a carry-on allowance of 3.4 ounces or less (the size of each container) for gravy, jam and jelly and maple syrup, and the release's list for those items does not mention one. This article does not decide which wording applies to a particular trip. For the 3.4-ounce rule itself, see [What Is the TSA 3-1-1 Liquids Rule? What's Exempt?](https://statesideexplained.com/tsa-3-1-1-liquids-rule/).

The release also names items the Food page has no row for: turkey, stuffing, casseroles and mac and cheese. The Food page's closest rows are Cooked Meat, Seafood and Vegetable (No Liquid), which allows carry-on and checked bags, and Soups, which allows 3.4 ounces (100 ml) or less in a carry-on. This article does not match the release's dishes to those rows.

## Who decides at the checkpoint

The Food page says the final decision rests with the TSA officer on whether an item is allowed through the checkpoint. Its rows for pies and cakes and cookies also say TSA officers may instruct travelers to separate items such as foods and powders from carry-on bags so they do not clutter bags and obstruct clear images on the X-ray machine.

## What TSA had published for the 2026 holiday

In 2025, TSA's national release had no food examples, and the holiday food examples were in local releases dated November 24 and 25. On November 4, 2026, TSA's press-release list showed no 2026 Thanksgiving release, and the newest item was dated October 28, 2026. If TSA publishes one, its examples may differ from the 2025 text above.

# Can You Bring Holiday Food Through TSA? What TSA Says About Thanksgiving Dishes

**TSA's Food page lists pies, cakes, cookies and cooked meat as allowed in both carry-on and checked bags. For gravy, jam, maple syrup and sauces it allows a carry-on only in containers of 3.4 ounces (100 ml) or less.** TSA's liquids page says the 3.4-ounce limit applies to each container, not to a total, and that these items go in a quart-sized bag. TSA's description of the 3-1-1 rule in a 2025 local release adds that each liquid or semi-liquid item must be in a container of 3.4 ounces or less and fit into a single quart-sized, clear, resealable plastic bag. TSA's local Thanksgiving releases of November 2026 list solid dishes such as pies, turkey, stuffing and casseroles for carry-on bags, and say cranberry sauce, gravy, canned goods in liquid, jams, jellies and maple syrup go in checked bags when they are liquid or spreadable and over 3.4 ounces. The Food page speaks to containers of 3.4 ounces or less in a carry-on and the releases to foods over 3.4 ounces, so the two statements cover different sizes. This article gives each with its own condition.

This article covers what TSA says about holiday food in three places: the Food list on TSA's "What Can I Bring?" pages; eight local TSA releases about Thanksgiving travel, seven dated November 23, 2026 (Salt Lake City, Phoenix Sky Harbor, Jacksonville, Dallas Fort Worth, Denver, Boston Logan and Hartsfield-Jackson Atlanta) and one dated November 18, 2026 (Harry Reid International Airport), whose food lists match; and TSA's "Liquids, Aerosols, and Gels Rule" page, used only to explain what the 3.4-ounce limit refers to. All of them were read on November 24, 2026, and the Food page and the Liquids page show no date. On that day TSA's press-release list showed no national Thanksgiving release, only these local ones. The article also compares the 2026 releases with a 2025 local release from Harrisburg International Airport. It does not cover airline rules, food import rules, alcohol or food safety.

## What TSA's Food page says

The Food page lists 59 items. Each has a carry-on value and a checked-bag value. None of the items is named cranberry sauce, stuffing, casserole or turkey. These are the rows that matter most for holiday food, with the values as the page shows them:

| Item on the Food page | Carry-on | Checked |
|---|---|---|
| Pies and Cakes | Yes | Yes |
| Cookies | Yes | Yes |
| Cooked Meat, Seafood and Vegetable (No Liquid) | Yes | Yes |
| Bread; Candy; Spices (dry) | Yes | Yes |
| Fresh Fruits and Vegetables | Yes (Special Instructions) | Yes |
| Fresh Meat and Seafood; Frozen Food | Yes (Special Instructions) | Yes |
| Gravy | Yes (Less than or equal to 3.4oz/100 ml allowed) | Yes |
| Jam and Jelly; Maple Syrup | Yes (Less than or equal to 3.4oz/100 ml allowed) | Yes |
| Salsa and Sauces; Soups | Yes (Less than or equal to 3.4oz/100 ml allowed) | Yes |
| Canned Foods | Yes (Special Instructions) | Yes |

Several rows say that solid food items (not liquids or gels) can be transported in either carry-on or checked bags. The Fresh Fruits and Vegetables row says this applies within the continental United States, and adds that passengers flying from Hawaii, Puerto Rico or the U.S. Virgin Islands to the mainland cannot take most fresh fruits and vegetables. This article does not go further into that point.

For fresh meat and frozen food, the page says that if the food is packed with ice or ice packs, they must be completely frozen when brought through screening, and that partially melted ice or ice packs with liquid at the bottom will not be permitted. For canned foods, the page says they could require additional screening that might result in the item not being allowed through the checkpoint, and TSA suggests packing them in a checked bag, shipping them or leaving them at home.

The Food page writes the limit as "Yes (Less than or equal to 3.4oz/100 ml allowed)" and does not say what the 3.4 ounces is measured against. TSA's "Liquids, Aerosols, and Gels Rule" page does. It says you are allowed a quart-sized bag of liquids, aerosols, gels, creams and pastes in a carry-on, limited to travel-sized containers that are 3.4 ounces (100 milliliters) or less per item, and that items in containers larger than 3.4 ounces or 100 milliliters go in checked baggage. The rule is about the size of each container, not a total for the trip. The page does not say anything about a larger container that is only partly full, and this article does not either.

The Liquids page does not say whether the quart-sized bag must be clear or resealable. The 2025 Harrisburg release, described below, words the same rule this way: each liquid or semi-liquid item must be in a container of 3.4 ounces or less and fit into a single quart-sized, clear, resealable plastic bag. So for gravy, jam and jelly, maple syrup, sauces and soups in a carry-on, the Food page's "Yes" comes with two conditions: a container of 3.4 ounces (100 ml) or less, and the quart-sized bag.

## What TSA's November 2026 local releases say

The eight releases carry the same food list. Seven of them put it under the heading "TSA holiday food guidelines: Carry-on vs. checked bags"; Phoenix Sky Harbor's release calls it "Thanksgiving food travel tips" and words the opening sentence a little differently, with the same meaning. The opening says solid items are permitted in carry-on bags, but any liquid, spreadable or pourable foods exceeding 3.4 ounces must be packed in checked luggage.

Under "Allowed in carry-on bags (solids)," the releases list: baked goods (holiday pies, cakes, cookies and brownies); meats (turkey, ham, chicken or steak, cooked, uncooked or frozen); stuffing (boxed mixes or prepared stuffing); casseroles (solid holiday casseroles that hold their shape); mac 'n' cheese (cooked in a pan, or dry box ingredients); and fresh produce (raw potatoes, yams, greens, apples, cranberries and citrus).

Under "Must be packed in checked bags (Liquids/spreads over 3.4 oz.)," they list: cranberry sauce (homemade jellied sauce or canned varieties); gravy (in jars, cans or plastic containers); canned goods (canned fruits or vegetables packed in liquid or syrup); and preserves (jams, jellies and maple syrup). The releases' lists do not separately describe containers of 3.4 ounces or less.

The releases also say that ice packs used to keep holiday foods cold must be completely frozen when passing through security, and that slushy or partially melted ice packs will not be permitted. They are local releases, and only these eight were read. This article does not say what other airports' releases contain.

## Compared with the November 2025 Harrisburg release

On November 24, 2025, TSA at Harrisburg International Airport (MDT) published a local release with a similar holiday food list. The two sets of lists are close. These are the points where the wording differs:

| Topic | Harrisburg release, Nov. 24, 2025 | Local releases, Nov. 2026 |
|---|---|---|
| How the limit is worded | Items that "can spill, spread, spray, pump, or pour" and are larger than 3.4 ounces go in checked luggage; no figure beside the checked-bag items | Liquid, spreadable or pourable foods exceeding 3.4 ounces go in checked luggage; the checked-bag list is headed "Liquids/spreads over 3.4 oz." |
| Cranberry sauce | Checked: "Homemade or canned, as it is spreadable" | Checked: homemade jellied sauce or canned varieties |
| Casseroles | Solid: carry-on or checked; casseroles with liquid or semi-liquid parts must follow the 3-1-1 liquids rule | Solid casseroles that hold their shape in carry-on; no casserole sentence about the 3-1-1 rule |
| Candy and spices | Listed as carry-on examples | Not listed |
| Ice packs | Permitted, frozen solid | Completely frozen; slushy or partially melted ice packs not permitted |

The 2026 releases do not say whether the 2025 wording still applies, and this article does not treat the 2025 text as current.

## The Food page and the 2026 releases, item by item

The Food page and the releases state different things about these items:

| Item | TSA Food page | Local releases, Nov. 2026 |
|---|---|---|
| Gravy | Carry-on: Yes, in containers of 3.4 oz (100 ml) or less, in the quart-sized bag. Checked: Yes | Checked list (liquids or spreads over 3.4 oz): gravy in jars, cans or plastic containers |
| Jam and jelly; maple syrup | Carry-on: Yes, in containers of 3.4 oz (100 ml) or less, in the quart-sized bag. Checked: Yes | Checked list (over 3.4 oz): preserves (jams, jellies and maple syrup) |
| Canned goods | Canned Foods: carry-on Yes (Special Instructions), which means they could require additional screening and TSA suggests a checked bag, shipping or leaving them at home; checked Yes | Checked list (over 3.4 oz): canned fruits or vegetables packed in liquid or syrup |
| Cranberry sauce | No row on the page | Checked list (over 3.4 oz): homemade jellied sauce or canned varieties |

The Food page says what is allowed in a carry-on: containers of 3.4 ounces or less, in the quart-sized bag, and it shows "Yes" for checked bags. The releases say what to pack in checked bags: liquids and spreads over 3.4 ounces. The two statements cover different sizes. The releases' lists do not separately describe containers of 3.4 ounces or less, and this article does not read that silence as a rule. The 2025 Harrisburg list showed no figure beside these items, so the 2026 heading states the size more clearly. For the 3-1-1 rule itself, see [What Is the TSA 3-1-1 Liquids Rule? What's Exempt?](https://statesideexplained.com/tsa-3-1-1-liquids-rule/).

The releases also name items the Food page has no row for: turkey, stuffing, casseroles, mac 'n' cheese and cranberry sauce. The Food page's closest rows are Cooked Meat, Seafood and Vegetable (No Liquid), which allows carry-on and checked bags, and Soups, which allows 3.4 ounces (100 ml) or less in a carry-on. This article does not match the releases' dishes to those rows.

The releases list fresh produce, and meats that are "uncooked, or frozen," under items allowed in carry-on bags. The Food page marks Fresh Fruits and Vegetables, Fresh Meat and Seafood and Frozen Food "Yes (Special Instructions)," and the instructions it gives for them are the Hawaii, Puerto Rico and U.S. Virgin Islands note, the ice rule above and, on the Fresh Meat and Seafood row, a dry ice note.

## Who decides at the checkpoint

The Food page says the final decision rests with the TSA officer on whether an item is allowed through the checkpoint. Its rows for pies and cakes and cookies also say TSA officers may instruct travelers to separate items such as foods and powders from carry-on bags so they do not clutter bags and obstruct clear images on the X-ray machine. The Food page also says "Contact AskTSA if your item is not listed."

## What this article does not cover

It does not cover airline rules, rules for bringing food into another country or into Hawaii beyond the one sentence above, alcohol, food safety, recipes or advice on how to pack. It describes what the TSA pages and releases listed under Sources state on the dates above and does not track changes to them. It does not say what a TSA officer will decide for a particular dish.

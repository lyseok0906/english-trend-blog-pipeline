# TSA Shoes Off and Laptops Out: What TSA Says by Lane

**It depends on the lane.** TSA announced in July 2025 that passengers at domestic airports can keep their shoes on at the checkpoint. For laptops, TSA's pages say they may stay in the bag in computed tomography (CT) lanes and PreCheck lanes, and that in a standard lane large electronics go into a bin.

This article covers what eight TSA pages say about shoes and large electronics: the national press release of July 8, 2025, the Travel Checklist, the Laptops, Tablets and Steel Toe Boots item pages, the Computed Tomography page, a local release of July 13, 2026 about CT units at Juneau, and the Security Screening page. The wording is as of November 9, 2026. It does not cover belts, jackets or liquids in detail, which airport has which equipment, children, travelers with medical needs or international airports.

## Shoes: what the July 2025 release says

TSA's national press release of July 8, 2025, headed "DHS to End 'Shoes-Off' Travel Policy," says the Homeland Security Secretary announced a policy that allows passengers traveling through domestic airports to keep their shoes on while passing through security screening at TSA checkpoints. Its subhead says passengers can now keep their shoes on at TSA security checkpoints. The release adds that other parts of TSA's layered approach still apply and that passengers must still clear identity verification, Secure Flight vetting and other processes.

The release does not say whether an officer can still ask a traveler to take off shoes, for example for additional screening, and this article does not fill that gap.

Two TSA item pages still tie shoes to PreCheck. The Laptops page (Last Updated on July 15, 2026) and the Steel Toe Boots page (Last Updated on May 26, 2022) both say TSA PreCheck travelers do not need to remove shoes, laptops, 3-1-1 liquids, belts or light jackets. Read alone, that sentence names shoes only for PreCheck travelers. The July 2025 release says shoes can stay on for passengers at domestic airports. TSA's pages do not say how the two fit together, and this article does not say which wording controls.

The Travel Checklist lists steps "In Standard Screening Lane": remove the 3-1-1 liquids bag and place it in the bin, empty the pockets and remove bulky jewelry, and remove personal electronic devices larger than a cell phone. It lists no step about shoes and shows no update date. Its packing tips mention shoes only as one layer in a bag packed in layers.

## Laptops and other large electronics in a standard lane

The checklist says to remove personal electronic devices larger than a cell phone from the carry-on bag and place them in a bin with nothing on or under them for X-ray screening, and it gives laptops, tablets, e-readers and handheld game consoles as examples. The Laptops page lists carry-on bags "Yes" and checked bags "Yes," and says to remove the laptop from the bag and place it in a separate bin for X-ray screening.

The Tablets page (Last Updated on July 21, 2026) lists carry-on bags "Yes" and checked bags "Yes" and points to the FAA regulations for portable electronic devices. It has no instruction about taking a tablet out of the bag, while the checklist names tablets among the devices to remove.

## CT lanes

TSA's Computed Tomography page describes CT as the latest checkpoint X-ray equipment for carry-on bags, which builds a 3-D image of a bag's contents. It says the goal is, in the future, to keep laptops and 3-1-1 liquids inside the bag during screening, and that under current screening procedures for this technology, laptops are allowed to remain inside the bag. The page shows no update date.

A local TSA release of July 13, 2026 about two new CT machines at Juneau International Airport says passengers using them are permitted to leave laptops and other electronic devices in their carry-on bags, and that passengers screened in lanes with CT units do not need to remove their travel-sized 3-1-1 liquids. It also says all carry-on items, including roller bags, go into a bin; that CT units have a slightly smaller entry tunnel, so not all larger carry-on bags fit; and that TSA recommends checking large carry-on items.

More on the liquids rule is in [What Is the TSA 3-1-1 Liquids Rule? What's Exempt?](https://statesideexplained.com/tsa-3-1-1-liquids-rule/), and more on the PreCheck lane is in [What Is TSA PreCheck? What It Changes at the Checkpoint](https://statesideexplained.com/tsa-precheck-what-it-changes/).

## What the pages do not say

The pages read here do not list which airports or lanes have CT units, so this article cannot say which lane a traveler will meet. The Juneau release is a local release about one airport. The checklist and the CT page show no date. The Laptops page, which names shoes only for PreCheck, is dated a year after the July 2025 release; the Steel Toe Boots page is dated before it. TSA says on its Security Screening page that it adjusts processes and procedures and that travelers may notice changes from time to time.

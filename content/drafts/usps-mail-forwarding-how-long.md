# How Long Does USPS Mail Forwarding Last?

**USPS says standard mail forwarding lasts 12 months.** After that, USPS says it returns mail to the sender for 6 months with a label that has your new address. A paid extension is possible for a permanent change of address.

This article covers what USPS's pages say. The wording is as of October 7, 2026, and comes from the three USPS pages listed at the end. It gives no prices and no advice on which option to choose.

## The 12 months and how it starts

USPS's forwarding page says standard mail forwarding lasts 12 months. It also says forwarding may begin within 3 business days of your request, but it is best to allow up to 2 weeks. USPS says identity must be verified to submit a change of address.

The USPS FAQ on forwarding options separates two kinds of request. A permanent change of address forwards mail for 12 months, while a temporary one forwards mail for a specified period of time. The forwarding page says a temporary change of address is for people relocating for 15 days up to 1 year.

## What gets forwarded

USPS says mail is forwarded piece by piece. Its forwarding page lists what is forwarded for free: First-Class Mail and periodicals, which it describes as newsletters and magazines, as well as Priority Mail Express, Priority Mail and USPS Ground Advantage. It says Media Mail is forwarded too, with the customer paying shipping from the local Post Office. It also says USPS Marketing Mail is not forwarded.

The two USPS pages do not describe periodicals the same way. The forwarding page says periodicals are forwarded for free and gives no separate time limit. The FAQ says a permanent change of address forwards "primarily First-Class Mail service for 12 months and Periodicals for 60 days". The FAQ adds that it generally does not forward USPS Marketing Mail or Package Services Mail. This article reports each page's wording as it stands.

## Extended Mail Forwarding

USPS offers a paid extension called Extended Mail Forwarding. Its FAQ says the extra time comes in 6-, 12- or 18-month increments, not to exceed a total of 18 months of extended forwarding time, in addition to the initial 12 months. Adding the two figures gives up to 30 months in all. That total is this article's arithmetic, not a number USPS states.

The FAQ says it is only available for a permanent change of address, and for a permanent, domestic request. It can be bought with the initial request. It can also be bought later, with the change-of-address confirmation code and the new ZIP Code. USPS says it sends a reminder email at the 11th month of a request. If someone buys 6 months, USPS says another 6-month interval can be added until the extension reaches 18 months. It says that once a change of address request has expired, the extension can no longer be bought.

USPS says an Extended Mail Forwarding request cannot be changed, and that canceling the change of address also cancels the extension, with no refund. Its forwarding page also says the extension cannot be canceled or refunded. The FAQ lists the classes that continue to be forwarded during the extension: First-Class Mail, USPS Ground Advantage Commercial items and Priority Mail.

## When forwarding ends

USPS says that when the forwarding period ends, it returns your mail to the sender for 6 months with a label that has your new address.

## What a change of address does not do

USPS's forwarding page says a change of address order only changes your mailing address with the Post Office. It says you must still update government agencies and companies yourself. For an absence of 3 to 30 days, the same page points to Hold Mail instead of forwarding. See [How Does USPS Hold Mail Work? Limits and Steps](https://statesideexplained.com/usps-hold-mail-how-it-works/) for that service.

## What this article does not cover

This article does not cover prices or fees, Premium Forwarding Service, business or military moves, mail for someone who has died, international moves, or identity-verification problems. It describes what USPS's pages state on the date above and does not track changes to them.

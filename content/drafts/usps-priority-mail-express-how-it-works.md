# USPS Priority Mail Express: What the 1-3 Day Guarantee Covers

**USPS pages describe Priority Mail Express (PME) as a 1-3 day service with delivery by 6 PM local time to most U.S. addresses and PO Boxes, backed by a money-back guarantee, and say the scheduled delivery day for a shipment depends on the ZIP Codes, the date and the time of day it is mailed.** An "Attempted delivery" scan can still count as meeting the commitment, the sender can choose a waiver of signature or require a signature, and an item sent as Hold for Pickup goes back to the sender if it is not picked up within 5 days, a count the pages word differently. This article compares what the pages say and shows where they word things differently.

This article uses the USPS FAQ "Priority Mail Express Service" (Article 000008250, dated May 22, 2026), the USPS.com page "Priority Mail Express" (no update date shown), the FAQ "What is a Waiver of Signature? What is Signature Required?" (Article 000006914, dated October 11, 2023) and the FAQ "Hold for Pickup Service" (Article 000008085, dated May 19, 2026). All four were read on November 24, 2026. It gives no prices, fees or insurance amounts, and it gives the refund rules only briefly: the full refund table is in our article on [USPS Refunds: What Can Be Refunded, When to Ask and What You Need](https://statesideexplained.com/usps-refund-how-it-works/).

## What the service is

The FAQ calls PME USPS's "fastest domestic shipping service" and says it delivers 7 days a week, 365 days a year, "with limited exceptions," to most U.S. addresses and PO Box addresses. It says the delivery is by 6 PM local time, and a note adds that the guaranteed 6:00 PM time is the local time at the recipient's address. The USPS.com page says Sunday and holiday delivery is available in many major markets for an additional fee, a fee this article does not give.

The FAQ says a PME item can be mailed at a Post Office, station or branch, mailed at a Contract Postal Unit, dropped into a PME collection box, handed to a carrier, or picked up through Package Pickup or Pickup on Demand. USPS.com gives the maximum weight as 70 pounds and the maximum combined length and girth as 108 inches.

## How the delivery day is set

The FAQ says the time of day a PME item is accepted by or dropped off to USPS, and other factors, affect whether it will be delivered in 1 to 3 days by 6 PM. It points to the Service Commitments tool, which it says shows the drop-off times and locations that meet the delivery time you want, the Scheduled Delivery Day and whether USPS can offer a money-back guarantee. The Scheduled Delivery Day is based on the ZIP Codes you are mailing from and to and the date and time you plan to mail the item. USPS.com's footnote says the scheduled delivery date depends on origin, destination and drop-off time, and that some restrictions apply.

The FAQ refers to the delivery commitment "found on your receipt." For the send-by dates USPS gives for Christmas, including the one for PME, see [USPS Holiday Shipping Deadlines 2026: Send-By Dates for Christmas](https://statesideexplained.com/usps-holiday-shipping-deadlines-2026/).

## What counts as meeting the commitment

The FAQ lists three scan events that meet the delivery commitment and adds "This is not a complete list."

- An "Attempted delivery" scan: the item was taken to the address, but the recipient was unavailable to accept it.
- An "Arrival at Pickup Point" scan, for PO Box and Firm Caller addresses: the item was available to the PO Box holder or caller, who did not come in to claim it.
- A "Forwarded" scan: the actual delivery address is different from the one on the label.

In the refund section the FAQ repeats that an attempted delivery meets the commitment and says other events also do. For what the tracking messages mean in general, see [USPS Tracking Status: What Each One Means and What USPS Says to Do](https://statesideexplained.com/usps-tracking-status-what-it-means/).

## Signature options

The Waiver of Signature FAQ says a waiver is started by the sender, must be checked at the time of mailing and cannot be changed in transit. With a waiver, the FAQ says the letter carrier signs for the domestic PME item as proof of delivery and leaves it in a secure location, if the area is secured and protected from weather. If the recipient does not claim the item within five days, it is returned to the sender. A waiver is not available with additional insurance, with PME Collect on Delivery (COD) or with PME COD Restricted Delivery. The FAQ also says a waiver voids insurance claims for loss of the package, while a claim can still be filed for damaged or missing contents or for service failures.

For the other choice, the FAQ says that on Label 11-F printed on or after January 2012 the sender who wants the addressee's signature must check the "signature required" box, or indicate it on a single-ply commercial label. If the box is checked, an image of the signature is provided to mailers who access delivery information. If it is not checked, the FAQ says the item can be left. A sender must select Signature Required for PME COD, PME COD Restricted Delivery and PME with additional insurance. Our [USPS Redelivery: How to Schedule It, Cutoffs and Return Dates](https://statesideexplained.com/usps-redelivery-how-it-works/) article lists domestic PME among the items that get a redelivery notice and reports the FAQ's wording about signature waiver.

## Hold for Pickup

The PME FAQ says a PME shipment can be sent as Hold for Pickup instead of having a carrier attempt delivery. It says items are held for five postal business days at the designated location and then returned to the sender, that the service is available at most retail locations and self-service kiosks, and that it is not available for PME items containing cremated remains. Notices go out by automated call, email or text, with a second automated notification after 3 days if needed or a final notification that the shipment will be returned after 5 calendar days. The Sunday and holiday fee is not applied to Hold for Pickup.

The Hold for Pickup FAQ adds that the addressee or designee must show an acceptable primary form of identification and that there is no fee for the service. For PME it says another notification attempt is made if the item is not picked up within 3 days, and the item is returned to the sender if it is not picked up within 5 days. It lists PME, with or without COD, among the items that can use Hold for Pickup at retail Post Offices, and PME among those available through Click-N-Ship. It excludes mail addressed to APO, FPO and DPO destinations such as PME Military Service.

## If it is late: a refund, not a claim

The FAQ says USPS refunds the postage and, if purchased, the Sunday or holiday premium fee in three cases: the item was not delivered by the guaranteed date and time given at the time of mailing, delivery was not attempted by then, or the item was not made available for pickup or claim by then. The sender must ask. A request can be made no sooner than 2 days after the date of mailing and no later than 30 days after, or between 30 and 60 days after if an Extra Service was purchased. The quickest way is online, or the sender can take the mailing receipt or the customer copy of the label to a Post Office and complete PS Form 3533. A denied request can be appealed, and an online request must be appealed online within 30 days of receiving the denial.

The FAQ lists exceptions, and the list below is not complete:

- The item was properly detained for law enforcement purposes, or delayed by a strike or work stoppage.
- The delay was caused by an incorrect ZIP Code or address, or forwarding or return service was provided after the item was made available for claim.
- The addressee made a written request that the item be held for delayed delivery, or the item was undeliverable as addressed.
- The item contained live animals or hazardous materials and was delivered, or delivery was attempted, within 3 days of mailing.
- A PME 1-Day shipment was mailed December 22 through December 25 and was delivered, or delivery was attempted, within 2 business days.
- For a refund requested for something other than loss, the item went to or came from Alaska, Hawaii, Guam, American Samoa or the Northern Mariana Islands, or went to Palau, the Marshall Islands or Micronesia.

A refund is different from an insurance claim. USPS.com says most PME shipments include insurance, with restrictions, and refers to sections 609.4.3, 609 and 503.4 of the Domestic Mail Manual for details. The Waiver of Signature FAQ ties claims for loss to the signature choice, as described above. For how a claim works, see [USPS Claims: Filing Periods, Who Can File and What You Need](https://statesideexplained.com/usps-claim-how-it-works/).

## Where the USPS pages differ

- **Refund window.** USPS.com says to submit a refund request for failure of service within 30 days of the mailing date. The FAQ gives the same 30 days and adds that the earliest time is 2 days after mailing and the latest is 60 days when an Extra Service was purchased.
- **Hold for Pickup time.** The PME FAQ says items are held "five postal business days," and also mentions a final notice after 5 calendar days. The Hold for Pickup FAQ says "5 days" for PME. The pages do not say whether these are the same count.
- **Delivery days.** The FAQ says PME delivers 7 days a week, 365 days a year, "with limited exceptions," and also says Sunday and holiday delivery is available in many major markets for an additional fee. It does not list the markets or the exceptions.
- **Age of the signature page.** The Waiver of Signature FAQ is dated October 11, 2023, and describes label editions from before and after January 2012. The PME FAQ is dated May 22, 2026. The pages do not say whether the label wording on the older FAQ is current.

## What the pages do not say

They do not say which major markets have Sunday and holiday delivery, or give a complete list of scan events that meet the commitment. They do not say what a sender sees on the receipt for the delivery commitment, beyond that it is "found on your receipt." They do not say how the Service Commitments tool treats a package mailed near a drop-off cutoff.

# USPS Refunds: What Can Be Refunded, When to Ask and What You Need

**USPS pages describe a refund as money back for postage or fees when a service's own refund conditions are met, for example when USPS is at fault, when more than the lawful price was charged, when a paid service was not provided, or when Priority Mail Express did not arrive by its guaranteed time.** The Domestic Mail Manual (DMM) sets application windows that run from 2 to 60 days after the mailing date, depending on the service, but a window does not by itself make a request eligible or approved. A claim for an insured item that was lost or damaged is a different request with its own rules.

This article compares three USPS sources: the USPS.com page "Request a USPS Refund: Domestic" (no update date shown), section 604.9 of the DMM on exchanges and refunds (no date), and the USPS FAQ "Domestic Refunds - The Basics" (Article 000005310, dated September 26, 2024), which only points readers to the USPS.com page. The wording is as of November 18, 2026. The sources overlap but are not identical, and the article shows where they differ instead of choosing one. It gives no prices, fees or refund percentages. It does not cover international refunds, business bulk refunds, postage meters and PC Postage in detail, Every Door Direct Mail, Premium Forwarding Service Residential or Postal Store returns.

## Refund or claim?

The two requests are easy to confuse because both can follow a lost package. The USPS.com refund page says that to file a claim for insured mail that was lost, damaged or missing contents, you should go to the File a Claim page. DMM 604.9.2 starts the same way: a customer may apply for a refund of postage and fees, and for an indemnity claim for loss or damage it points to section 609.

| | Refund | Claim |
|---|---|---|
| What it is | Money back for postage or fees when a service's own refund conditions are met, such as USPS at fault, too much charged, a paid service not provided, or a Priority Mail Express guarantee missed (DMM 604.9.2 and 604.9.5; USPS.com) | For insured mail that was lost, damaged or missing contents (USPS.com claims page; DMM 609) |
| Where the rules are | USPS.com refund page; DMM 604.9 | USPS.com File a Claim page; DMM 609 |
| Windows | Set by service; a window is a time limit for applying, not a guarantee; see the table below | Their own filing periods; see our [USPS claims article](https://statesideexplained.com/usps-claim-how-it-works/) |

The USPS claims FAQ, as our claims article reports, says postage reimbursement is included with the claim payment if the item was lost or not delivered, and that insurance fees "are not reimbursed." The DMM's list of cases with no refund includes the fees for insured mail, COD, Priority Mail Express insurance and Registered Mail once USPS has accepted the article. A Missing Mail search is neither a refund nor a claim; our [Missing Mail article](https://statesideexplained.com/usps-missing-mail-search-how-it-works/) covers it.

## When to apply, by service

The DMM chart (Exhibit 9.2.1) gives the windows below, counted from the mailing date. The USPS.com page repeats some of them and adds service-by-service steps. A window is only the period in which a request may be made. It does not mean the request is eligible or will be approved: each service has its own conditions and exceptions, which are set out on the [USPS.com refund page](https://www.usps.com/help/refunds.htm) and in [DMM 604.9](https://pe.usps.com/text/dmm300/604.htm) (section 604.9.2 for postage and extra services, 604.9.5 for Priority Mail Express), and the main exceptions are summarized under "What is not refundable" below.

| Service | What USPS says about timing (a window, not a guarantee) | How to apply |
|---|---|---|
| Priority Mail Express, no extra service | 2 to 30 days after mailing (DMM chart and USPS.com); eligibility depends on the guaranteed arrival time on your receipt and the DMM 604.9.5 exceptions | Online with a free USPS.com account, or at a Post Office with PS Form 3533 |
| Priority Mail Express with an extra service | 30 to 60 days after mailing; one combined request | Same |
| Extra services such as Certified Mail, Return Receipt, Signature Confirmation | 30 to 60 days after mailing (DMM chart and USPS.com) | Online for some; otherwise where you paid |
| Postage on other mail when no service was rendered | 10 to 60 days after mailing (DMM chart only; the USPS.com page does not list this row) | PS Form 3533 at a Post Office (DMM 604.9.2.5) |
| Unused Click-N-Ship label | Counted from the print date; the online route also needs an unscanned label with no earlier refund request; see the next section | Your Click-N-Ship account, or email the Help Desk |
| PO Box fees | USPS.com: new unused box, within 30 days of payment; box in use, pro-rated | Online for a new box; the Post Office where the box is, for a box in use |
| Package Intercept, Pickup On Demand | No window given on the page | Online request |

Our [Certified Mail article](https://statesideexplained.com/usps-certified-mail-how-it-works/), [PO Box article](https://statesideexplained.com/usps-po-box-how-it-works/), [Package Intercept article](https://statesideexplained.com/usps-package-intercept-how-it-works/) and [Package Pickup article](https://statesideexplained.com/usps-package-pickup-how-it-works/) cover each of those services in more detail.

For Pickup On Demand, the page says you are eligible only if the carrier fails to show up during the specified day, and not if the carrier shows up or if you cancel after 5 a.m. local time on the pickup day. For Priority Mail Express, the page says the service comes with a money-back guarantee, that postage is eligible for mail that did not arrive by the guaranteed arrival time shown on your receipt, that each tracking number can be used for only one refund request, and that refunds for the service and related extra services must be combined in a single request. DMM 604.9.5.1 says that, unless a situation in 604.9.5.5 delayed the item, USPS refunds the postage and any Sunday or holiday premium fee for an item that was not delivered, for which delivery was not attempted, or that was not made available for claim by the delivery date and time specified at mailing.

## Click-N-Ship labels: what the 30 and the 60 days mean

The USPS.com page says unused Click-N-Ship labels are eligible for refunds up to 60 days after the print date, which it also calls the label transaction date. It then gives two routes. In your Click-N-Ship account you open Shipping History, check the labels, choose Refund Labels and proceed. The page lists three conditions for this route: the label has not been scanned by the postage system, it was created within the past 30 days of the label print date, and it does not already have a refund request. For a label printed more than 30 but less than 60 days ago, the page says to email the Click-N-Ship Help Desk with your username, Click-N-Ship account number, label number, and transaction number and date.

So on the USPS.com page, the 30 days belong to the online route in your account and the 60 days to the older-label route by email, and both are counted from the print date, not the mailing date. The page's own wording leaves a small gap: "up to 60 days" can include day 60, while the email route says "less than 60 days." The page does not say what applies on day 60, and this article does not either. Timing alone does not make a label eligible: the online route also requires that the label has not been scanned and has no earlier refund request.

DMM 604.9 does not name Click-N-Ship in any rule on label refunds; its only mention is a Sunday-fee refund for USPS Connect Local that is applied automatically through Click-N-Ship. It does have 30-day and 60-day rules in 604.9.3.10 and 604.9.3.11, but they are for PC Postage indicia: with a package identification code, a request must be started within 30 days of printing and sent electronically to the PC Postage provider; without one, the labels must be sent physically to the provider within 60 days of the date in the indicia. The DMM does not say that Click-N-Ship is a PC Postage product, so this article does not apply those two rules to Click-N-Ship, and it does not say the two sets of numbers are the same rule. The 60-day figure for meter indicia (604.9.3.2) is a third, separate rule.

## What to bring

The USPS.com page says you will need proof of purchase and that you may need some or all of: the tracking number, the purchase receipt and a photo ID if you apply in person. For extra services and Priority Mail Express it lists the tracking number and the mailing receipt, and for an in-person Priority Mail Express request it says to fill in Part I of PS Form 3533 in duplicate and bring the original customer copy of Label 11.

The DMM adds rules about evidence. For an online request, the mailer must keep the evidence of the services purchased until the request is resolved, and may be asked to upload it as a .jpeg, .pdf or .png file and give the mailing date. For PS Form 3533, the mailer submits the envelope or wrapper (or a part of it) showing the sender and addressee names and addresses, canceled postage and postal markings, or other evidence of postage and fees paid. The DMM also says no refund is made for postage, or for fees for extra services not rendered, when a postmarked mailing receipt, a retail Post Office mailing receipt or valid USPS acceptance or mail processing scan events are not available.

## What is not refundable

Being inside a window does not make a request eligible, and these exceptions apply even then. DMM 604.9.2.4 lists cases with no refund. They include COD, Priority Mail Express insurance, insured mail and Registered Mail fees after USPS accepts the article, even if the article is later withdrawn; unused adhesive stamps, which may be exchanged instead; undeliverable Certified Mail and Signature Confirmation fees when a scan shows an attempted delivery; and the Package Intercept per-piece fee once USPS successfully intercepts the mailpiece.

DMM 604.9.5.5 lists cases where Priority Mail Express postage is not refunded, including a delay caused by a strike or work stoppage, an item that is undeliverable as addressed, a shipment held at the addressee's written request, and a 1-Day shipment mailed December 22 through December 25 that was delivered or attempted within two business days. For items to or from Alaska, Hawaii and some territories, it says postage is not refunded when the request is for something other than loss. Our [holiday shipping deadlines article](https://statesideexplained.com/usps-holiday-shipping-deadlines-2026/) shows how two USPS pages footnote the Priority Mail Express guarantee for the holiday season.

## How a refund is paid and what if it is denied

The USPS.com page says approved online refunds receive a check by mail, approved refunds requested at the Post Office are paid in cash, check or money order, approved Click-N-Ship refunds are credited to the original payment account, and business customers who applied online are refunded by check. The DMM adds that for requests on PS Form 3533, USPS may refund the postage immediately, in cash or with a no-fee money order, when it can tell right away that the mailer is entitled to it and the item has postage affixed (up to a limit the DMM states, not repeated here), and otherwise researches the request and pays by check or credit to an account.

If a refund is partly paid or denied, USPS.com says you may file a dispute within 30 days of receiving the decision. DMM 604.9.2.5 says appeals of denied online requests must be made online within 30 days of receipt of the denial; for PS Form 3533, it says adverse rulings may be appealed through the postmaster to a USPS manager and gives no number of days. So for refunds both sources count from receipt. For the claims appeal, our claims article shows that the sources count the 30 days from different points.

## Where the sources differ

- **Extra-service windows.** The DMM chart and the USPS.com page count the 30 to 60 days from the mailing date. DMM 604.9.2.4 lists no refund for extra-service fees when the request is made less than 30 or more than 60 days from the date the service was purchased. The pages do not say how the two dates fit together.
- **PO Box refunds.** USPS.com says that for a box in use you can request a pro-rated refund for unused time at the Post Office where the box is, and that for a new box applied for online you can request a refund online within 30 days of the original payment. The USPS FAQ "PO Box - The Basics" (Article 000005315, dated November 13, 2026) instead sets refunds by rental term and month, says a 3-month box is not eligible once activated, and says a box never activated gets an automatic full refund after 30 days of inactivity. The DMM sends PO Box fee refunds to a different section (508.4.6), which this article did not read.
- **Package Intercept.** The USPS.com page says the Package Intercept fee, plus any applicable postage charge, may be refundable if the shipment was not intercepted. The DMM says the per-piece fee is not refunded once USPS successfully intercepts the mailpiece. What is charged when an item is not intercepted is covered in our Package Intercept article, and this article does not reconcile the wording.
- **Postage on other mail.** The 10-to-60-day row appears only in the DMM chart; the USPS.com page does not list it, so the page alone does not tell a customer who sent an ordinary letter or package when to apply.
- **The FAQ.** The refund FAQ is a short pointer to the USPS.com page and is dated September 26, 2024, so it adds no rule of its own.

## What the pages do not say

For the services covered here, the USPS.com page gives a processing time only for Package Intercept and Pickup On Demand: normally 2 to 3 business days, longer if USPS needs more information, with a credit that may take up to two billing cycles to appear on the card. It gives no processing time for Click-N-Ship, extra-service or Priority Mail Express refunds. This article gives no fee or refund amount for any service.

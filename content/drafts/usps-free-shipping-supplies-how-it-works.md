# USPS Free Boxes and Envelopes: What You Can Order and the Rules That Apply

**The USPS Postal Store lists free Priority Mail and Priority Mail Express boxes and envelopes, plus free labels and forms, and its product pages say the packaging is free when you pay for the matching service and may only be used to ship that way.** The pages also describe how the supplies are shipped to you and give a separate phone line for ordering them. This article goes through what the pages list, the rules they state, and where they word things differently.

This article uses the Postal Store "Free Shipping Supplies" listing (47 results when read, no update date shown), nine Postal Store product pages, the USPS.com "Contact Us" page (no update date shown) and the USPS FAQ "Ordering Free Shipping Supplies" (Article 000007007, dated September 29, 2026), all read on November 26 and 27, 2026. Product pages show a price field and some state shipping or postage prices; this article gives no amounts. The product pages are individual store entries, not a policy page, so wording can change as items are added or retired.

## What the listing includes

The listing shows free boxes and envelopes for Priority Mail, Priority Mail Flat Rate and Priority Mail Express, including padded, legal, window and poly envelopes, several box sizes, a shoe box and a cube-shaped box. It also lists address, shipping and hold-for-pickup labels, forms such as Certified Mail receipt forms, domestic return receipt forms and Collect on Delivery forms, customs declaration form pouches, military supplies such as an APO/FPO Flat Rate box and a Military Care Kit, and cremated remains kits. The items are shown at no cost.

Most items come in packs. The product pages say that an order quantity of 1 equals the whole pack, for example 10 envelopes or 10 or 25 boxes depending on the format you choose, and the Priority Mail poly envelope page adds that USPS is moving from Tyvek to poly, so you may receive either one while Tyvek is in stock.

## The rule on the product pages

The pages for the boxes and envelopes say the same thing in three versions. For Priority Mail, the poly envelope and shoe box pages say the envelopes and boxes are free when you pay for Priority Mail shipping service and may only be used to ship via Priority Mail. For Flat Rate, the Medium Box page says the supplies are free when you pay for Priority Mail Flat Rate shipping and may only be used to ship via Priority Mail Flat Rate. For Priority Mail Express, the Cremated Remains Kit page says the envelopes and boxes are free when you pay for Priority Mail Express and may only be used to ship via Priority Mail Express.

These pages also list what a shipment includes: USPS Tracking with most shipments, insurance with most shipments (restrictions apply) and a free Package Pickup at your home or office, which our article [How Does USPS Package Pickup Work? Free Pickup and Limits](https://statesideexplained.com/usps-package-pickup-how-it-works/) covers. If a Priority Mail or Priority Mail Express envelope or box is used for international shipping, the pages say it is charged at the international price for that service.

## Special items: cremated remains kits, labels and forms

The Cremated Remains Kit 1 page says the free kit holds one Priority Mail Express box and a roll of Priority Mail Express tape, and that Priority Mail Express is the only way for a person or organization to ship cremated remains. It points to Publication 139 for packaging and shipping guidelines. The listing's Kit 2 adds bubble cushioning, a self-sealing plastic bag and Publication 139 to the box and tape.

The forms and labels are not packaging, and the pages read for them do not repeat the "only used to ship via" sentence. The Certified Mail receipt forms page says the receipt is for domestic mailing only and gives the sender a mailing receipt and, on request, electronic verification of delivery or a delivery attempt; our article [What Is USPS Certified Mail? What It Includes and How It Works](https://statesideexplained.com/usps-certified-mail-how-it-works/) covers the service. The Registered Mail labels page says the maximum is one roll per order and that larger orders should go through your local Post Office; our article [USPS Registered Mail: Security, Tracking and Delivery Explained](https://statesideexplained.com/usps-registered-mail-how-it-works/) covers that service. The customs form pouch page says the pouch is used with Form 2976-A or Form 6182.

## Ordering, quantities and delivery

Each product page has a quantity field that shows a minimum and a maximum, and the maximums differ by item and by format. The pages do not give one overall limit.

The Shipping & Returns text on the product pages says that in-stock orders ship with USPS Tracking, that you should allow one business day from order receipt for them to ship, and that free shipping is by USPS Ground Advantage, with delivery in 2 to 5 days depending on distance, at no cost to the customer. The USPS FAQ "Ordering Free Shipping Supplies" says orders are shipped within one business day from order receipt and words the delivery as an expected 2 to 5 business days, so this article uses business days for that figure. Both pages describe an expedited option by Priority Mail, with an expected delivery of 1 to 3 business days according to the FAQ, for a fee whose amount this article does not give. The product description above those tabs adds a different line, which the "Where the pages differ" section covers. The pages add that orders may ship from different distribution centers and may arrive separately, that every order carries a "Carrier - Leave if No Response" endorsement so the carrier can leave it if you are not home, and that you can see order status and tracking in your USPS.com account under Activity History. The Postal Store does not accept orders for shipment outside the United States but ships to APO, FPO and DPO addresses and U.S. territories.

The Contact Us page lists a phone line, 1-800-610-8734, "to order free shipping boxes, envelopes, and postal forms, or to report a problem with your shipping supply order." It gives Monday to Friday 7 AM to 11 PM Eastern Time and Saturday 7 AM to 6 PM Eastern Time. Our article [USPS Customer Service: How to Phone, Email or File a Complaint](https://statesideexplained.com/usps-customer-service-how-to-contact/) covers the other lines.

## Where the pages differ

- **Time to ship.** The Shipping & Returns text asks you to allow one business day from order receipt for in-stock items to be shipped, while the FAQ says orders are shipped within one business day from order receipt. The product description says to allow 5 to 7 business days for in-stock items to be shipped, and also says Ground Advantage supplies ship within 2 to 5 business days. The FAQ gives an expected delivery of 2 to 5 business days after that shipping step, while the Shipping & Returns text says 2 to 5 days depending on distance. The pages do not reconcile these.
- **The Dual-Use tube.** The Dual-Use Priority Mail / Priority Mail Express Small Tube Box page says the box can be used for either service. It does not carry the "how it works" sentence that ties the other boxes to one service, and it says the packaging is subject to the nonstandard fee because of its size. The page does not say which service's payment makes it free.
- **The APO/FPO box.** The Priority Mail Flat Rate APO/FPO Box page also lacks the "free when you pay" sentence. It describes military shipping at domestic rates and mentions a discounted price for that box, which this article does not give.
- **Similar boxes that are not free.** The comparison table on the Flat Rate Medium Box page lists prepaid versions of similar boxes with a box price, so a similar name does not mean the box is free.

## What the pages do not say

They do not say whether you need a USPS.com account to place an order, whether the quantity maximums apply per order or per day, or what happens if a free box is used with a different service than the one it is tied to. This article read all three listing pages (47 results) and nine product pages, not every item or a general Postal Store shipping policy.

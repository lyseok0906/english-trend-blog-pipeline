# Do You Need a REAL ID to Fly? What TSA Accepts

**You need an ID that is on TSA's list, and a REAL ID is one of them.** TSA says adults 18 and older must show valid identification at the airport checkpoint. A state license or ID card must be REAL ID compliant to be on the list. A U.S. passport is also on the list.

This article covers what TSA's pages say. The wording is as of October 7, 2026, and comes from the three TSA pages listed at the end. TSA says its list "is subject to change without notice" and encourages travelers to check it again before traveling.

## What TSA says about REAL ID

TSA says REAL ID enforcement began on May 7, 2025. It says that as of that date, state-issued driver's licenses and IDs that are not REAL ID compliant are no longer accepted as valid identification at airports. TSA's REAL ID page says U.S. travelers must be REAL ID compliant to board domestic flights and to access certain federal facilities.

TSA's identification page says that passengers should either travel with an acceptable alternative form of ID, like a passport, or enroll for a state-issued REAL ID through their state Department of Motor Vehicles (DMV). If you are not sure whether your ID complies with REAL ID, TSA says to check with your state DMV. TSA also says a temporary driver's license is not an acceptable form of identification.

## Examples of IDs on TSA's list

TSA's list has many more entries than these. The examples below are taken from it.

| ID | What TSA says |
|---|---|
| State-issued REAL ID-compliant driver's license or ID card | On the list |
| State-issued Enhanced Driver's License or Enhanced ID | On the list |
| State-issued license or ID that is not REAL ID compliant | No longer accepted at airports as of May 7, 2025 |
| Temporary driver's license | Not acceptable |
| U.S. passport or U.S. passport card | On the list |
| DHS trusted traveler card (Global Entry, NEXUS, SENTRI, FAST) | On the list |
| U.S. Department of Defense ID, including IDs issued to dependents | On the list |
| Permanent resident card | On the list |
| Photo ID issued by a federally recognized Tribal Nation | On the list |
| Mobile driver's license | TSA accepts certain ones issued by states approved for federal use; the mobile license must be based on a REAL ID, an Enhanced Driver's License or an Enhanced ID |

## Expired ID and children

TSA says it currently accepts expired ID up to two years after expiration, for the forms of identification on its list.

TSA says it does not require children under 18 to provide identification when traveling within the United States. It adds that unaccompanied minors who are eligible for TSA PreCheck must show an acceptable ID to receive expedited screening, and it tells families to contact the airline about its own ID requirements for travelers under 18.

## If you do not have an acceptable ID

TSA says that starting February 1, 2026, a traveler who cannot provide an acceptable ID at a checkpoint has the option to pay a fee to use TSA ConfirmID, and that TSA will then attempt to verify the traveler's identity. TSA describes ConfirmID as a fee-based service. It says the process takes an average of 10 to 15 minutes, but could take 30 minutes or more. TSA also says that if your identity cannot be verified, you will not be allowed to enter the screening checkpoint.

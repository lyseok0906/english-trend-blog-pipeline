# What Is USPS Informed Delivery? How the Free Preview Works

**Informed Delivery is a free, optional U.S. Postal Service (USPS) service that shows you digital previews of your incoming letter-sized mail and status updates for your packages.** USPS says it digitally images the front of letter-sized mail as it runs through automated sorting equipment, and uses those images for notifications before the physical mail arrives.

This article explains what USPS's own pages say. The wording is as of October 7, 2026, and comes from two USPS pages listed at the end. It describes the service and does not recommend signing up.

## What it shows

USPS says the images show only the exterior, address side of letter-sized mailpieces. Its Informed Delivery page describes them as grayscale.

| Item | What USPS says |
|---|---|
| Letter-sized mail | The Daily Digest email shows images of up to 10 pieces. The online dashboard has no limit on the number of images shown and displays mail images for a seven-day period. |
| Catalogs and magazines | Not imaged by USPS's automated equipment, so they do not appear in the email or dashboard. |
| Packages | Status updates for packages arriving at your address and packages you have sent. USPS says package information is shown as status updates, not images. |

## How the notifications arrive

USPS says notifications are delivered by Daily Digest email, on the online dashboard and in the USPS Informed Delivery mobile app. The email is sent once a day, typically before 9:00 AM local time, Monday through Saturday. USPS says notifications are not sent on days when there is no mail to be delivered, on Sundays or on federal holidays. An account can have only one email address, but USPS says multiple accounts can have Informed Delivery for the same address.

## A preview is not a delivery date

USPS says Informed Delivery provides notifications for mailpieces arriving soon, not necessarily mailpieces arriving that same day. It asks people to allow several days, up to one week, before reporting a mailpiece as missing. USPS also says images may not match the mail actually delivered that day, for example when automated equipment is moved or shut down for maintenance or when pieces of mail overlap as the image is taken.

## Who can sign up

USPS says Informed Delivery is available to most residential and PO Box addresses and many business addresses. In rare cases a person cannot sign up even in an eligible ZIP Code, because the individual mailbox is not uniquely coded. USPS says this is more common in high-density buildings such as apartment buildings and condos.

Signing up means creating or using a USPS.com account and verifying your identity. USPS says that if identity cannot be verified online, the person can request a code by mail, which USPS says arrives within 3 to 7 postal business days, or verify in person at a Post Office. This article does not walk through the steps.

## Why did I get a letter about Informed Delivery?

USPS says a Welcome Letter is sent as part of its mail-based verification of new Informed Delivery accounts. If you recognize the account information in the letter, USPS says no further action is required.

If the account was not created by you or someone in your household, or you do not recognize the masked username or email address, USPS says to deactivate it by following the instructions in the letter. Those instructions use an unsubscribe code printed in the letter, which USPS says is case-sensitive and must be entered exactly as it appears. USPS says that once the code is submitted, the account is unenrolled and access is disabled. It says the code expires 90 days after the date of issue, after successful use, or if the profile address is edited.

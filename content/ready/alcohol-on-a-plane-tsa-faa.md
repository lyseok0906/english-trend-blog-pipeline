# Can You Bring Alcohol on a Plane? What TSA and the FAA Say

**TSA says alcoholic beverages are allowed in checked bags and in carry-on bags, but carry-on amounts are limited to 3.4 oz (100 ml).** In checked bags, TSA says beverages with more than 24% but not more than 70% alcohol are limited to 5 liters (1.3 gallons) per passenger and must be in unopened retail packaging. TSA lists alcoholic beverages with more than 70% alcohol (over 140 proof) as not allowed in either carry-on or checked bags.

This article covers what TSA's and the FAA's pages say. The wording is as of October 13, 2026, and comes from the five pages listed at the end. It describes the rules and does not give advice on what to pack.

## What TSA says for carry-on and checked bags

| Where | What TSA says |
|---|---|
| Carry-on bag | Allowed in amounts of 3.4 oz (100 ml) or less |
| Checked bag, 24% alcohol or less | Not subject to limitations (TSA sets no separate alcohol limit for checked bags) |
| Checked bag, more than 24% but not more than 70% alcohol | Limited to 5 liters (1.3 gallons) per passenger, in unopened retail packaging |
| Carry-on or checked bag, more than 70% alcohol (over 140 proof) | Not allowed |

For carry-on bags, TSA's page adds that mini bottles of alcohol must be able to comfortably fit into a single quart-sized bag. For the general carry-on liquids rule, see [the TSA 3-1-1 liquids rule](https://statesideexplained.com/tsa-3-1-1-liquids-rule/).

## The 5-liter limit is a total per passenger

For beverages with more than 24% but not more than 70% alcohol by volume (up to 140 proof), the FAA's PackSafe pages say they must be in unopened retail packaging, with a limit of 5 liters per packaging and 5 liters in total per passenger. The regulation says the same: unopened retail packagings not exceeding 5 liters, and a total net quantity per person of 5 liters. So the 5 liters is a total for each passenger, not a limit per bottle. TSA's page words it as 5 liters (1.3 gallons) per passenger for checked bags. A footnote on the FAA's page says liquids, gels and aerosols in carry-on baggage are further limited to 100-ml (3.4 oz) containers at the TSA security checkpoint.

## Beverages with 24% alcohol or less

TSA's page says beverages with 24% alcohol or less are not subject to limitations in checked bags. That is TSA's statement about checked bags only. It does not change the carry-on limit above, and it does not speak for any airline's own policy. The regulation, 49 CFR 175.10(a)(4), covers beverages with not more than 24% alcohol by volume and does not state a quantity limit for them.

## Beverages with more than 70% alcohol

TSA has a separate "What can I bring?" entry for alcoholic beverages over 140 proof. It defines them as alcoholic beverages with more than 70% alcohol (over 140 proof), including grain alcohol and 151 proof rum, and lists both carry-on bags and checked bags as "No." The FAA's PackSafe chart, updated November 14, 2025, has a row for alcoholic beverages over 140 proof, defined as greater than 70% alcohol by volume (more than 140 proof), and marks them as not allowed in carry-on or checked bags. The FAA's separate web page on alcoholic beverages has the same heading and refers to the regulation, but its text does not state a rule. The regulation's exception covers beverages up to 70% alcohol by volume.

## Drinking alcohol on board

TSA says FAA regulations prohibit travelers from consuming alcohol on board an aircraft unless it is served by a flight attendant. The FAA's page says passengers may not drink alcohol on board unless it is served by the air carrier. TSA adds that flight attendants are not permitted to serve a passenger who is intoxicated.

## Your airline and the TSA officer

TSA says to check with your airline before bringing any alcoholic beverages on board. TSA's pages also say the final decision rests with the TSA officer on whether an item is allowed through the checkpoint.

## What this article does not cover

This article does not cover duty-free purchases, flights arriving from other countries, any airline's own policy, or state and foreign laws about alcohol. It does not cover alcohol carried for sale, how to pack bottles, or how much to bring. This article describes what the five pages state on the date above and does not track changes to them.

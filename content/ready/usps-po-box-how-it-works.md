# How Does a USPS PO Box Work? Sizes, ID and Service Periods

**A USPS PO Box is a secure, numbered box at a Post Office where your mail is kept until you collect it.** USPS says you can apply online or at a Post Office, and that you pick up your keys or lock combination at the Post Office where the box is, after the staff verify your identification. The rental fee depends on the box size and the location. This article does not give fees.

This article covers what three official USPS sources say: the USPS.com page "Rent a PO Box", the USPS FAQ "PO Box - The Basics" (dated October 16, 2026), and section 4.0 of the Domestic Mail Manual (DMM 508), which sets the rules for PO Box service. The wording is as of November 6, 2026. The USPS.com page and the DMM show no update date. The article does not cover prices, free box service, Caller Service or business accounts.

## Sizes

USPS.com says most Post Offices have five PO Box sizes, depending on availability, and the DMM says the sizes available vary by facility. The page describes what each size holds; the DMM gives capacities in cubic inches. Both give the same frontal dimensions, though they list the width and height of sizes 3 and 5 in a different order.

| Size | Front (inches) | What USPS.com says it holds | DMM capacity |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 (XS) | 3 x 5.5 | 10-15 letters and 3 rolled magazines | Under 296 cubic inches |
| 2 (S) | 5 x 5.5 | 15+ letters, 5 rolled magazines, or 1 Small Priority Mail Flat Rate Box | 296 through 499 |
| 3 (M) | 5.5 x 11 | Large envelopes and magazines stacked, and 2 Small Priority Mail Flat Rate Boxes | 500 through 999 |
| 4 (L) | 11 x 11 | Small and Medium Priority Mail Flat Rate Boxes, with room for 10-15 letters | 1,000 through 1,999 |
| 5 (XL) | 12 x 22.5 | Big enough for multiple packages | 2,000 or more |

## How to get one

USPS.com describes four online steps: search for Post Offices near you, choose a location, box size and payment period, complete the online application (which generates PS Form 1093) and submit it with payment, then print the form and take it to the Post Office counter where the box is, with 2 forms of acceptable U.S. identification, within 30 days of applying online. After the retail associate verifies your identity, you get your keys or lock combination. To apply in person, you bring your payment and 2 forms of acceptable U.S. identification to the Post Office.

The DMM describes identification in more detail. Each applicant listed on the form, including both spouses and other individuals listed but not minors, must present two items of valid, current identification: one acceptable primary form and one acceptable secondary form. The DMM refers to its sections 608.10.3 and 608.10.4 for what counts, and this article does not list them. Spouses may complete and sign one form. Minors may be provided with service unless a parent or guardian objects in writing, but they cannot apply online.

You can rent a box in a different city or state, but USPS.com says you still have to visit the Post Office where the box is, with the proper documentation, to pick up the keys or combination.

## If the box you want is not available

USPS.com says you can choose another size, look at another location, or ask to be put on a wait list for that size at that location. USPS says it will contact you by email or phone when the box becomes available, and that you then have 2 days to reserve it before it goes to the next person on the list. The DMM says the postmaster may refer you to another facility, use a waiting list, provide general delivery until a box is available, or offer a different size or Caller Service, and that online customers cannot apply for a box that is not available but can join the waiting list.

## Service periods, payment and renewal

USPS.com lists terms of 3, 6 and 12 months, and the USPS FAQ lists 3-month, 6-month (semi-annual) and 12-month (annual) increments. The DMM says in 4.1.7 that PO Box service is available in 3-, 6- or two 6-month (semi-annual) prepaid periods, and in 4.4.3 that a fee may generally be paid up to one year in advance. None of the pages says in so many words how a 12-month term relates to two 6-month periods, so this article does not call the wording a conflict and does not say which terms a given Post Office offers.

All three say the 3-month option involves automatic recurring renewal. USPS.com adds that a 3-month term has no opt-out, while with a 6- or 12-month term you can opt out of automatic renewals. The DMM adds that the 3-month option is not available at locations on the April/October payment schedule.

USPS.com lists four ways to pay: online with a credit or debit card, at a self-service kiosk, by mail (a check or money order payable to "U.S. Postal Service," sent to the Postmaster where the box is), or in person. You pay for a full rental period at the beginning.

The pages describe the payment date and late payment in different words. USPS.com says payments must be made by the 10th of the month the payment is due, and that if you do not pay, the box is closed on the 11th of that month. The USPS FAQ says that if payment has not been received by the 10th day after the due date, the service is terminated and incoming mail is returned to senders, and that box fees are due on either the 15th or the last day of the month, depending on the customer's billing cycle. The DMM gives no closing date: it says a renewal fee may be paid during the last 30 days of the service period but no later than its last day (4.4.4), and that the lock replacement fee also applies as a late payment charge when a box is renewed more than 10 days after the renewal due date (4.7.5). The DMM also lists failing to pay by the due date as one of the cases in which a box is surrendered. The pages do not say how these dates fit together, so this article does not give one deadline; the Post Office where the box is can tell you which date applies to your box.

If you close a box, USPS.com says you may be eligible for a refund, and that the amount depends on how long you have had the box. The USPS FAQ sets refund conditions by term (for example, it says a box on a 3-month term is not eligible for a refund once it is activated), and the DMM sets refund rules by service period in 4.6. See [USPS's refund details](https://faq.usps.com/s/article/PO-Box-The-Basics#refund) for the current conditions.

## Keys and access

USPS.com says you get two keys, or the combination, when you open a box, and the DMM says two keys are initially issued to each new box customer. USPS.com says more keys cost an additional fee. Most locations allow 24/7 access to the box, although USPS.com says hours vary by Post Office. The DMM says mail meant for a box must show the PO Box number in the address immediately above the city, state and ZIP Code.

## Packages and signatures

If a package does not fit in the box, USPS.com says you will either get a slip to pick it up at the counter during normal business hours, or get it from a secure parcel locker, including USPS Smart Lockers at select locations. For deliveries that need a signature, USPS leaves a notice in the box and you sign at the counter; with the free Signature on File service, USPS.com says it leaves the item in the box instead.

USPS.com also describes Street Addressing, where available: you can use the street address of your Post Office together with your PO Box number, which lets you receive packages from private carriers such as UPS, FedEx, DHL and Amazon, as long as they comply with USPS mailing standards.

## Closing a box

USPS.com says you can close a box online through Manage PO Box or by telling the retail associate at the Post Office where the box is, and that you should then submit a Change of Address form, after which mail can be forwarded to your new address for up to one year. The DMM lists a written notice to discontinue service and a permanent change-of-address order among the cases in which a box is surrendered.

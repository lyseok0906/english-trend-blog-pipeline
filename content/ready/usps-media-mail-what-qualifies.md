# What Can You Send by USPS Media Mail? What Qualifies

**USPS says Media Mail is for a short list of items: books, films, printed music, recordings and a few educational and medical materials.** USPS.com says that if you include anything that is not allowed, you or your recipient will have to pay higher postage rates, and that all Media Mail packages are subject to opening and inspection. USPS.com gives a maximum weight of 70 pounds.

This article covers what two USPS pages say: the Media Mail section of the USPS.com Mail & Shipping Services page and section 173 of the Domestic Mail Manual (DMM), "Media Mail and Library Mail." The wording is as of November 9, 2026. Neither page shows an update date. The article gives no prices or fee amounts and instead links USPS's price calculator and price list, and it does not cover Library Mail in detail, commercial mailings, international mail or how a borderline item would be treated at a Post Office.

## What qualifies, item by item

USPS.com lists eight kinds of items. The DMM, which says "only these items may be mailed at the Media Mail prices," adds the conditions. The table puts the two side by side.

| Item | USPS.com list | What DMM 173 adds |
|---|---|---|
| Books | Books (at least 8 pages) | At least eight printed pages; wholly reading matter or scholarly bibliography, or reading matter with incidental blank spaces for notations; no advertising other than incidental announcements of books |
| Films | 16-millimeter or narrower width films | Positive prints in final form for viewing; films and film catalogs sent to or from commercial theaters do not qualify |
| Printed music | Printed music and test materials | Printed music in bound or sheet form |
| Test materials | Printed music and test materials | Printed objective test materials and their accessories, used by or on behalf of educational institutions |
| Recordings | Video and sound recordings (tapes, albums, CDs and DVDs) | Sound recordings, with video recordings and player piano rolls classified as sound recordings; includes incidental announcements and guides or scripts for use with them |
| Playscripts and manuscripts | Playscripts and manuscripts | Playscripts and manuscripts for books, periodicals and music |
| Educational reference charts | Printed educational reference charts | A single printed sheet whose information is conveyed mainly by graphs, diagrams, tables or other nonnarrative matter; a chart conveyed mainly by narrative text does not qualify |
| Medical loose-leaf pages | Medical loose-leaf pages and binders | Medical information for distribution to doctors, hospitals, medical schools and medical students |
| Computer-readable media | Computer-readable media | Containing prerecorded information, and guides or scripts prepared solely for use with such media |

## What does not qualify

USPS.com adds a note that video games, computer drives and digital drives do not qualify for Media Mail prices. The DMM does not mention video games. Its list for computer-readable media covers media containing prerecorded information and guides or scripts for use with them, and this article does not say how a particular disc or drive would be treated.

Other limits come from the DMM list in the table: books must have at least eight printed pages and no advertising beyond incidental book announcements; films and film catalogs sent to or from commercial theaters do not qualify; and a chart conveyed mainly by narrative text does not qualify as an educational reference chart. The DMM also says matter that is mailed or required to be mailed as First-Class Mail, or that is entered as Periodicals, is not Media Mail.

## Inspection

USPS.com says all Media Mail packages are subject to opening and inspection by the Postal Service. The DMM says Media Mail and Library Mail are not sealed against postal inspection, and that mailing articles at Media Mail prices constitutes the mailer's consent to postal inspection of the contents, regardless of how the package is closed.

## Weight, size and fees

USPS.com gives a maximum weight of 70 pounds and sends readers to the Media Mail eligibility standards (the DMM section above) for the full list of requirements. It also says you will pay additional fees for a package that is large and lightweight, has a nonstandard size, shape or material, or has perishable items. The DMM says Media Mail pieces are charged per pound or fraction thereof, and that any fraction of a pound counts as a whole pound. This article gives no amounts. For prices, USPS.com links its [Price Calculator](https://postcalc.usps.com/), and the DMM refers readers to [Notice 123, the USPS price list](https://pe.usps.com/text/dmm300/Notice123.htm).

## Delivery time

USPS.com shows "Shipping in 2-8 Days" for Media Mail, with a footnote that service to or from destinations outside the contiguous U.S. may be affected by limited transportation. The DMM says USPS does not guarantee the delivery of Media Mail or Library Mail within a specified time. Both statements are on official USPS pages. The pages do not say whether the USPS.com range is an estimate, so this article does not read it as a promise, and it does not say which statement controls.

## Enclosures and writing on the package

The DMM sets extra rules. A book may have one envelope or one addressed postcard bound into its pages and one order form, and may include incidental announcements of books. Printed matter that is mailable as USPS Marketing Mail may be included loose with qualifying material. An invoice that relates only to the matter mailed may go inside the piece or in an envelope marked "Invoice Enclosed" attached to the outside.

On writing, the DMM says markings that have the character of personal correspondence require, with certain exceptions, additional postage at First-Class Mail prices. The exceptions include the sender's and addressee's names and addresses, marks describing the contents, instructions for using the item, and words or phrases such as "Do Not Open Until Christmas" and "Happy Birthday, Mother." Every piece must show a delivery address with the correct ZIP Code or ZIP+4 code, and the sender's return address.

## Library Mail

USPS.com calls Library Mail a related service for eligible organizations, including schools, museums, nonprofits and other groups. DMM section 173 covers both services, and its Library Mail rules (section 5.0) name the eligible senders and addressees. This article does not cover them.

## What the pages do not say

Neither page shows an update date. The old USPS.com Media Mail page now shows "This Page Has Moved" and points to the Mail & Shipping Services page. The pages do not settle borderline items, so for an item that is not on the list, a Post Office is the place to ask.

# Federal Holidays in 2027: The Full List and Observed Dates

**The U.S. Office of Personnel Management (OPM) lists 11 federal holidays for 2027, from Friday, January 1 to Friday, December 24.** Three of them are printed on a different day from their calendar date: Juneteenth on Friday, June 18, Independence Day on Monday, July 5 and Christmas Day on Friday, December 24.

This article gives OPM's 2027 list with the weekday of each date, the rule in federal law that sets each holiday, and the three moved days. It also keeps one more date, Friday, December 31, 2027, apart from the list, because OPM files it under 2028. It is about federal employees only. The facts are as of November 4, 2026, and come from the pages listed at the end. OPM's page shows no update date.

## The 2027 list

The table follows OPM's "2027 Holiday Schedule." The last column is the rule in section 6103(a) of title 5 of the United States Code, the law OPM names as the one that sets holidays for federal employees. OPM prints the day that is treated as the holiday, not always the calendar date, so the weekdays of the three calendar dates that were moved (June 19, July 4 and December 25) are computed from the calendar and are not OPM's.

| OPM's date | Holiday | Rule in 5 U.S.C. 6103(a) |
|---|---|---|
| Friday, January 1 | New Year's Day | January 1 |
| Monday, January 18 | Birthday of Martin Luther King, Jr. | Third Monday in January |
| Monday, February 15 | Washington's Birthday | Third Monday in February |
| Monday, May 31 | Memorial Day | Last Monday in May |
| Friday, June 18 | Juneteenth National Independence Day | June 19, a Saturday in 2027 |
| Monday, July 5 | Independence Day | July 4, a Sunday in 2027 |
| Monday, September 6 | Labor Day | First Monday in September |
| Monday, October 11 | Columbus Day | Second Monday in October |
| Thursday, November 11 | Veterans Day | November 11 |
| Thursday, November 25 | Thanksgiving Day | Fourth Thursday in November |
| Friday, December 24 | Christmas Day | December 25, a Saturday in 2027 |

OPM's footnote on Washington's Birthday says that name is the one in section 6103(a), and that OPM's policy is to always refer to holidays by the names in the law. Other institutions may use other names. For Thanksgiving, see [When Is Thanksgiving 2026 in the U.S.? How the Date Is Set](https://statesideexplained.com/thanksgiving-2026-date/) for how the fourth Thursday is set.

## How the dates are set

Six of the 11 holidays in the statute are set by a weekday rule: the birthdays of Martin Luther King, Jr. and Washington, Memorial Day, Labor Day, Columbus Day and Thanksgiving. The other five are set by a calendar date: New Year's Day, Juneteenth National Independence Day, Independence Day, Veterans Day and Christmas Day. In 2027 the three moved days are all among the five that have a calendar date, because June 19, July 4 and December 25 fall on a weekend.

## The three moved days

OPM's footnote on those rows says that if a holiday falls on a Saturday, for most federal employees the preceding Friday will be treated as a holiday for pay and leave purposes, and that if it falls on a Sunday, the following Monday will be. It cites 5 U.S.C. 6103(b) for the Saturday rule and Section 3(a) of Executive Order 11582 for the Sunday rule. The explanation and the examples for 2026 to 2028 are in [When a Federal Holiday Falls on a Weekend, What Changes?](https://statesideexplained.com/federal-holiday-weekend-observed/), which this article does not repeat.

For 2027 that gives Friday, June 18 for Juneteenth, Monday, July 5 for Independence Day and Friday, December 24 for Christmas Day.

## The December 31, 2027 date

OPM's 2028 table starts with Friday, December 31, for New Year's Day, marked with the same weekend footnote. New Year's Day 2028, January 1, is a Saturday, so the Friday before it, December 31, 2027, is the observed date for New Year's Day 2028. The date falls in 2027, but OPM does not print it on the 2027 tab, and this article does not count it among the 11 holidays above. The 2028 tab prints no year in that row; the year is worked out from the weekday.

## What this article does not cover

This article does not cover what state or local governments, banks, the stock market, schools, the Postal Service or private employers do on these days. Each of them sets its own schedule, and this article uses no source for any of them. It also does not cover pay and leave details, other work schedules, or OPM's "In Lieu Of" fact sheet. The statute also names Inauguration Day, January 20 of each fourth year after 1965, as a legal public holiday for certain employees; it is not a 2027 date and is not on OPM's 2027 list. This article describes what OPM and the statute state on the date above and does not track changes to them.

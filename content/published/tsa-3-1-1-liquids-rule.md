# What Is the TSA 3-1-1 Liquids Rule? What's Exempt?

**The TSA liquids rule lets you bring liquids, aerosols, gels, creams and pastes in your carry-on bag if each container holds 3.4 ounces (100 milliliters) or less and the containers go in a quart-sized bag.** TSA says to pack anything in a larger container in checked baggage.

This article explains what TSA's own pages say. The wording below is as of October 7, 2026, and comes from the two TSA pages listed at the end. Neither page shows an update date or a notice of change, so they do not say when the wording last changed. This article does not either.

## What the rule says

TSA's rule page says you are allowed to bring a quart-sized bag of liquids, aerosols, gels, creams and pastes in your carry-on bag and through the checkpoint. The items must be in travel-sized containers of 3.4 ounces (100 milliliters) or less per item. TSA's FAQ refers to this as the 3-1-1 liquids rule.

For containers larger than 3.4 ounces, TSA says to pack them in checked baggage.

## What to expect at the checkpoint

TSA says that placing these items in the small bag and separating it from your carry-on baggage "facilitates the screening process." The page does not say that you must take the bag out at every checkpoint, so this article does not say so either.

TSA also says that any liquid, aerosol, gel, cream or paste that alarms during screening requires additional screening.

## What is exempt

TSA's rule page links to three exemptions: medications, infant and child nourishments, and inbound international flights. This article covers the last two. It does not cover medications.

### Breast milk, formula and juice

TSA's FAQ says that formula, breast milk and juice in quantities greater than 3.4 ounces (100 milliliters) are allowed in carry-on baggage and do not need to fit in a quart-sized bag. TSA says to remove these items from your carry-on bag so they can be screened separately from the rest of your belongings.

### Duty-free liquids on inbound international flights

TSA's rule page says duty-free liquids over 3.4 ounces (100 milliliters) may be carried in a secure, tamper-evident bag if all three of these are true:

1. You bought them internationally and you are flying to the United States on a connecting flight.
2. The retailer packed them in a transparent, secure, tamper-evident bag with no sign of tampering.
3. The original receipt is with the items and the purchase was made within 48 hours.

The items must still be screened and cleared. TSA says an item that alarms or cannot be screened is not permitted in the carry-on. TSA recommends packing all liquids over 3.4 ounces in checked baggage, even if they are in a tamper-evident bag. It also says that liquids over 3.4 ounces that are not in a secure, tamper-evident bag must be packed in checked baggage.

## What this article does not cover

This article does not cover medications, specific airports, other countries' liquid rules or TSA PreCheck. It describes the rule as TSA's pages state it on the date above and does not track announcements about changing it. TSA's pages are the place to confirm the current wording before you fly.

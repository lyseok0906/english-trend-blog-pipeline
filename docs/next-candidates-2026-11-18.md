## Recommended candidate: USPS refunds (what can be refunded, when to ask, what to bring)

- **Working title (proposal):** USPS Refunds: What Can Be Refunded, When to Ask and What You Need. **Slug (proposal):** `usps-refund-how-it-works`. **Focus keyword (proposal):** `usps refund`. All three are proposals for the user to confirm.
- **Reader problem:** a customer paid for postage, a label or an extra service and the service was not performed or the item was not used; which of these USPS says it refunds, within what window, with which documents, online or at the Post Office, and how the refund is paid.
- **Official source, opened 2026-11-18 in the built-in browser:** USPS.com "Request a Domestic Refund" (https://www.usps.com/help/refunds.htm; no update date shown; 11,561 characters). It lists, per service, what is eligible, when to apply and what to bring: unused Click-N-Ship labels (online refund for labels printed within 30 days; older labels up to 60 days by email to the Click-N-Ship Help Desk); extra services such as Certified Mail, Return Receipt and Signature Confirmation (30 to 60 days after the mailing date; tracking number and mailing receipt); Priority Mail Express (2 to 30 days after the mailing date without extra services, 30 to 60 days with them; money-back guarantee tied to the guaranteed arrival time on the receipt; Form 3533 at the Post Office); PO Boxes; Package Intercept; Pickup On Demand; EDDM Retail; Premium Forwarding Service Residential; Postal Store returns. After approval: online refunds are paid by check, Post Office refunds in cash, check or money order, Click-N-Ship refunds go back to the original payment account; a denied or partly paid request can be disputed within 30 days of receiving the decision. It points to the Domestic Mail Manual 604.9.2 and 604.9.5 for eligibility details (https://pe.usps.com/text/dmm300/604.htm), which still have to be read, and to the claims page for lost or damaged insured mail.
- **Overlap with published posts (checked in the repository, 2026-11-18):** the Missing Mail post mentions that a money-back-guarantee service may be refundable and sends readers to the claims and refund pages; the claims post says USPS.com sends postage and fee refunds to a separate page that "this article did not read" (the refund page); the Certified Mail, PO Box, Package Intercept and Package Pickup posts each cover one refund rule of their own service in a short section. No published post explains the refund page as a whole, and none covers Click-N-Ship label refunds, the refund windows by service, how a refund is paid or the dispute period. The new post would link to those five posts for the per-service detail and keep its own section on each service to what the refund page says, so it would act as the overview rather than repeat them. Not a duplicate, but the most connected candidate so far; the PO Box, Intercept and Pickup rules must not contradict what those posts already say (to be checked claim by claim at Gate 1).
- **Shown, not resolved (expected):** Click-N-Ship "up to 60 days" against the 30-day online window; the dispute clock ("within 30 days of receiving the decision") against the wording on the claims FAQ and DMM 609 ("from the date of the original decision") already shown in the claims post; the Priority Mail Express money-back guarantee against the holiday-shipping-deadlines post's footnotes.
- **Out:** every dollar amount and fee (the page states some; none is used), international refunds (separate page, not read), business bulk refunds beyond one sentence.
- **Search interest:** the broad phrase "usps refund" about 2.1 x the tracking-status phrase; the specific phrases are small. Search volume not checked.
- **Official-source access:** the page and the DMM are public and opened without a block; a USPS FAQ article on refunds has not been searched yet (the FAQ search page did not render results) and would be looked for at drafting.

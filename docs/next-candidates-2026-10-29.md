### A. Lighters on a plane (`lighters-on-a-plane-tsa`, lane: airport security rules)

- **Official sources (all read in full 2026-10-29):** five TSA pages, each with a header line and little text: disposable and Zippo (carry-on Yes; checked Yes with special instructions; "Lighters with fuel are prohibited in checked bags, unless they adhere to the Department of Transportation exemption, which allows up to two fueled lighters if properly enclosed in a DOT approved case"; last updated July 14, 2026), arc/electronic (carry-on Yes with special instructions, checked No; prevent unintentional activation; each lithium ion battery 100 Wh or less, lithium metal 2 g; no recharging on board; Nov 4, 2024), torch (No / No; Oct 10, 2024), lighter fluid (No / No; Oct 10, 2024), lithium battery powered (carry-on Yes, checked No; Oct 10, 2024). FAA PackSafe "Lighters" (updated April 13, 2026): liquid-fuel lighters without an absorbent lining forbidden; absorbed-liquid and butane lighters limited to **one lighter per passenger** in carry-on or on one's person; lithium battery powered lighters carry-on only; torch lighters not allowed in the cabin or in checked baggage by both the Hazardous Materials Regulations and TSA rules. 49 CFR 175.10(a)(2): "one packet of safety matches or a lighter intended for use by an individual when carried on one's person or in carry-on baggage only"; lighter fuel and refills and lighters with unabsorbed liquid fuel not permitted anywhere; battery-lighter conditions. eCFR up to date as of 10/27/2026.
- **Source tension to handle:** the TSA disposable page says checked bags **Yes** with a DOT exemption for up to two fueled lighters in an approved case, while the FAA web page and the regulation say lighters go on the person or in carry-on only. The FAA lighters FAQ PDF (dated November 18, 2013; read only through a summarizing fetch tool on 2026-10-29, so **not yet read directly**) explains the exemption as DOT special permits for travel containers, domestic travel only, and says torch lighters in those containers may go in checked baggage, which differs from the TSA torch page (No / No) and the FAA web page. An article must attribute each statement and say where the pages differ.
- **Overlap with existing posts:** none on lighters. Adjacent to the power-bank post (battery limits) and the TSA 3-1-1 post; internal links, not duplication.
- **Reader question:** clear and practical ("can I bring a lighter / Zippo / vape lighter / torch lighter on a plane"). Competition (search tool, indicative only): airline-claims and luggage-brand blogs.
- **Difficulty: high.** Five TSA pages plus FAA page plus regulation plus a 2013 FAQ that conflicts on one point; DOT special permit detail; the lighter type decides the answer. Needs a type-by-type table, a short diagram and a full Gate 1 on 25 or more claims; Gate 2 will probe the FAQ conflict.
- **Demand check:** relative interest only (Trends, about 60 in the comparison above, the highest of the five phrases). Volume unknown.
- **Time pressure:** none (evergreen). Page dates differ (2024 to 2026).
- **Re-check:** mandatory, six pages; the FAA PDF (FAQ and, if used, the printable chart) opened directly.
